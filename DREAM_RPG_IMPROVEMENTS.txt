CENOTAPH DREAM RPG IMPROVEMENT ROADMAP
======================================

Purpose
-------

This file collects high-leverage improvements for turning Cenotaph into the
surreal vertical RPG survival looter shooter described by the README:

  Stable Core + Expandable Content + Dreamlike Presentation

The goal is not to add every feature at once. The goal is to make each new
system strengthen the climb, survival pressure, relic hunting, buildcraft,
replayability, and the mystery of the mountain.


Guiding Target
--------------

Cenotaph should become a game where the player thinks:

  "I survived that ascent because of the relics I chose, the route I risked,
  the Anchor I reached, and the way I adapted when the mountain changed."

And also:

  "I understood what the mountain asked me to do. I do not yet understand why
  it felt like something I had failed to do before."

The best improvements should make the mountain feel older, taller, stranger,
more dangerous, and more mechanically expandable.


Canon Reading Order
-------------------

The full documentation reread established this authority order:

- Identity contract: `docs/design/IDENTITY_CONTRACT.md` protects genre, tone,
  production, art, audio, and core-loop identity.
- Narrative truth: the current working-tree `README.md` says the Pilgrim is the
  former king who fled judgment, ordinary Cycles repeat his failure, and true
  victory is atonement and release.
- Current slice: `docs/design/ASH_WALK_PILGRIMAGE.md` owns the exact Warden,
  reward, Anchor, and mountain-reaction sequence.
- Supporting design: `LORE.md`, `SYSTEMS.md`, `CONTENT_GUIDE.md`, and
  `ROADMAP.md` provide tone, systems, content, and staging. Their mysteries are
  in-world uncertainty, not permission to contradict developer-facing truth.
- Archive: `docs/archive/` is historical and explicitly superseded.

The king's exact sin and final payment remain author-level mysteries. Systems
may support their later definition, but must not invent either one.


Thematic Integration Contract
-----------------------------

Theming is not replacing a familiar noun with a strange noun. A feature belongs
in Cenotaph when its mechanic, source, decision, response, and memory all express
the same part of the mountain.

Every meaningful player-facing feature should answer five questions:

- Mechanical truth: what does it do, cost, risk, and permit?
- World ownership: which Stratum, rite, relic, witness, enemy, route, or wound
  makes it exist?
- Player decision: what must the Pilgrim choose, refuse, carry, surrender, or
  survive?
- Mountain answer: what readable world, HUD, ambience, particle, dialogue, or
  encounter state responds?
- Remembrance: what can the current Ascent, next Cycle, or final reckoning
  remember about the choice?

A feature that answers only with color, ornate naming, lore text, or particles
is decorated, not integrated.


Clarity Boundary
----------------

Internal code, schemas, validators, editor fields, logs, and debug commands
should keep precise engineering names such as `objective_id`, `loot_table_id`,
`damage_kind`, and `save_version`. The player-facing layer may use ritual
language only when the action remains immediately understandable.

Use a clear action first and an evocative implication second:

  BREAK THE LAST CHAIN
  The road above still carries its weight.

Do not hide damage, cost, duration, failure, route direction, accessibility, or
save state behind poetry. The player may misunderstand history, never the rule
they are currently being asked to obey.


Player-Facing Language Grammar
------------------------------

Use these as presentation families, not forced replacements for every internal
term:

- Health -> Vessel. Keep exact health available where it affects a decision.
- Stamina -> Breath. Sprint, dash, climb, and exhaustion remain plain actions.
- Unsecured carried resource -> Exposed Ash.
- Banked resource -> Bound Ash.
- Current objective -> Pilgrimage, with a direct authored action line.
- Objective history -> Pilgrimage Journal or Remembrance.
- Inventory -> Reliquary, while `Equip`, `Compare`, and `Discard/Refuse` remain
  explicit commands.
- Build/loadout -> Attunement, showing every contributing relic, rite, curse,
  and stat source.
- Perk or upgrade choice -> authored Rite, Vow, or Attunement only when its cost
  and effect remain visible.
- Status effect -> authored Affliction, Burden, Mark, Silence, or Blessing with
  a plain mechanical description and duration.
- Boss/elite -> authored name and title. Never reduce a named keeper or witness
  to a generic `BOSS` label.
- Run summary -> Remembrance.
- New Game Plus -> New Cycle.
- Replaying prior state -> Remember an Earlier Echo.
- Ordinary end-of-Cycle result -> Coronation Failure.
- True campaign victory -> Final Release.

Settings, accessibility, save recovery, error messages, and developer tools
should remain direct. Theme may frame them, but must never make them harder to
operate.


Recurring Symbol Grammar
------------------------

Recurring symbols need stable semantic jobs or they become decorative noise:

- Ash: memory made material, exposure, loss, and what can be bound.
- Chains: obligation, imprisoned continuity, forced ascent, and routes held by
  unfinished duty. Breaking a chain must change more than a door mesh.
- Bells: warning, judgment, ritual summons, and dead places calling. They use
  silence until developer-approved recordings exist; never synthesize a bell.
- Mirrors: false memory, delayed recognition, alternate paths, and identity.
  Reserve their strongest uses for Mirror-Crust and later Cycles.
- Wind: altitude, exposure, scale, and pressure from what cannot be seen.
- Silence: suppression, removed agency, interrupted prayer, and absence. It is
  not a generic stealth or mute effect.
- Tarnished gold: failed authority, temptation, royal debt, and dangerous
  importance. Do not use it as universal rarity confetti.
- Cold Anchor light: temporary claim, memory held in place, defense, and ritual
  permission.
- Blood and ember: immediate bodily danger, damage, aggression, and unstable
  survival.
- Roots and machinery: obligations and systems continuing after their purpose
  or ruler has vanished.
- Waystones and processional roads: orientation, authored passage, and the
  difference between being guided and being welcomed.

One feature should normally have one primary symbol and at most one supporting
symbol. Using every Cenotaph motif at once weakens all of them.


Stratum Escalation Grammar
--------------------------

The same system should change thematic meaning as the Pilgrim climbs:

- Ash-Walk: physical ruin, exposed survival, first claims, and understandable
  consequences.
- Ward of Irons: authority continuing as purposeless machinery and confinement.
- Hanging Slums: lives abandoned below royal decisions and survival over void.
- Sanctuary: relief without belonging, preparation, and incompatible testimony.
- Gallery of Wind: scale, exposure, and the Pilgrim's insignificance.
- Mirror-Crust: recognition, contradiction, and identity becoming unavoidable.
- The Breach: judgment, recurrence, Coronation Failure, and possible release.

Do not spend a later Stratum's strongest symbol, revelation, enemy logic, or
surreal rule in Ash-Walk merely because it looks dramatic. Each feature needs a
home Stratum, allowed mutations, and a spoiler tier.


Presentation Authority
----------------------

Thematic hooks may specify semantic color, motion, timing, ambience role,
particle behavior, text tone, and world consequence. They do not authorize an
AI or coding pass to invent production geometry, anatomy, ornament, relic forms,
music, recorded voices, or one-shot sounds. Honest primitives and silence remain
the correct fallback until the developer supplies direction or assets.


The Missing Center: Goal and Victory
------------------------------------

The current build is a strong systems test, not yet a game the player can win.
The player can enter Ash-Walk, fight, gather Ash and relics, bind an Anchor, and
walk through a return gate, but the game never states a complete purpose,
recognizes success, or reaches a terminal outcome. The return gate currently
loads `foundation_test` on proximity even if the pilgrimage is unfinished.

That is the most important design gap in the project.

Cenotaph can remain mysterious and deeply replayable while still being
beatable. Mystery should delay the truth of the Pilgrim's identity and sin, not
obscure the immediate task or whether a declared milestone was completed.

Use this layered victory contract:

- Immediate goal: survive the current danger and reach the next meaningful rite.
- Route goal: overcome or outwit the pressure that guards the chosen passage.
- Stratum goal: complete an authored Rite of Passage and open the way upward.
- Prototype completion: finish Ash-Walk's authored pilgrimage and receive an
  honest test-slice completion state.
- Ordinary Cycle outcome: reach the upper mountain, face its reckoning, and
  fail Coronation in a way that reflects what the Pilgrim still refuses to
  understand or surrender. The failure remains meaningful and remembered.
- True campaign victory: across Cycles, recover enough truth, witness the
  kingdom's suffering, complete abandoned obligations, accept the king's
  identity without reclaiming his authority, relinquish something of real
  value, and remain for judgment when escape would be easier.
- Final ending: pay for the sin, end the recurrence, let the kingdom and
  mountain finally die, and roll credits. The true victory is release, not
  possession.
- Post-credits play: revisit an earlier remembered Echo or start another
  profile. Do not canonically restart the Cycle after the final release.

The early player-facing promise should avoid the identity revelation while
remaining clear:

  "Climb the impossible mountain. Bind its Anchors. Survive what guards each
  passage. Recover what the mountain cannot remember. Reach The Breach."

The hidden developer-facing destination is equally clear:

  "Remember the king who fled. Become answerable to the throne without taking
  it back. Remain for judgment, pay the debt, and release the Cenotaph."

For the current test build, add a smaller honest victory:

  Enter Ash-Walk -> break the Last Chain -> recover the Warden's relic
  -> commune with the First Anchor and choose a rite
  -> deliberately cross the awakened continuation gate
  -> FIRST PILGRIMAGE REMEMBERED

This is not the final ending. It is a complete vertical-slice outcome that lets
the player understand the goal, complete the test pilgrimage, see what their
choice changed, and choose to replay or continue testing. Binding, mending, or
turning away must remain a real authored choice; the outcome should remember
which rite the player chose instead of requiring a claim that erases the choice.


Highest Priority Improvements
-----------------------------

Audit sequencing note:

The full codebase audit later in this file refines implementation order. Items
1-6 remain high-value goals, but the lifecycle, damage/fall correctness,
schema-runtime parity, multi-level persistence, shared simulation boundary, and
stable runtime-entity work in items 51-60 should be treated as prerequisites
where they conflict with feature sequencing. The goal and victory work in items
111-121 is not late-roadmap polish: define it first, and implement the small
Ash-Walk completion state alongside the foundation corrections. Items 122-130
are cross-cutting theme rules: apply them as each feature is built rather than
scheduling them as a late cosmetic phase.

1. Make Ash-Walk worth replaying before adding another Stratum

   The current Ash-Walk slice is the right proving ground. Do not answer weak
   replayability with more map. First, make this one compact pilgrimage feel
   worth repeating.

   Add:

   - One real route split: high exposed path versus lower oppressive path.
   - One route-specific hazard or encounter.
   - One route-specific reward table.
   - One reason to replay the route after binding the First Anchor.
   - One small secret that appears only under a cycle condition.

   Success test:

   - The same Ash-Walk ascent can be described differently across two runs.
   - The player makes at least one route decision before the Warden.
   - Death before binding still feels costly, but not unfair.


2. Separate authored level data from mutable runtime entity state

   This is the most important architecture improvement.

   Currently, authored props and runtime facts live too close together:
   enemy health, dynamic positions, removed props, pickups, generated loot, and
   event consequences all lean on the prop list.

   Add:

   - Stable runtime entity handles.
   - A runtime entity table built from authored LevelData.
   - Separate components for health, transform, loot, enemy AI, interaction,
     Anchor state, and persistence identity.
   - A clean conversion step:

       LevelData -> RuntimeWorld -> Physics/Render/HUD/Save snapshots

   Why this matters:

   - Saves become safer.
   - Enemy and loot systems become easier to expand.
   - Encounters can spawn entities without mutating authored content directly.
   - Future bosses, status effects, affixes, and world scars become much easier.


3. Move gameplay and physics to a fixed-step schedule

   The game already caps delta time, which helps. The next reliability step is
   fixed-step simulation with bounded substeps.

   Add:

   - A fixed update rate for movement, physics, cooldowns, enemy attacks, and
     combat logic.
   - A separate variable visual interpolation pass.
   - Deterministic time accounting for tests and replay.

   Why this matters:

   - Movement feels consistent across machines.
   - Enemy attacks become easier to tune.
   - Combat bugs become reproducible.
   - Input recording and headless scenario tests become realistic.


4. Add headless gameplay scenario tests

   The project already has good validation and unit tests. The next confidence
   jump is testing the actual ascent loop.

   Add tests for:

   - Spawn into a level.
   - Move and jump.
   - Shoot and kill an enemy.
   - Collect resource.
   - Pick up a relic.
   - Trigger a manual enemy defeat event.
   - Bind an Anchor.
   - Die and respawn at the active Anchor.
   - Transition between levels.
   - Save, continue, and reconstruct world state.

   Why this matters:

   - It protects the dream from boring regressions.
   - It lets the game grow without becoming fragile.


5. Add encounter definitions

   Enemy props are enough for the foundation, but dream-RPG survival needs
   encounters that feel authored, reactive, and replayable.

   Add data for:

   - Encounter ID and display name.
   - Spawn groups.
   - Enemy roles.
   - Elite modifiers.
   - Reward tables.
   - Event hooks.
   - Encounter budget.
   - Route or cycle conditions.
   - Optional named encounter HUD.

   Example:

   - "Last Chain Procession"
   - Two Ashbound, one Censer, one Burdened elite.
   - Appears on the low route when Ash Memory is active.
   - Rewards Sovereign relic bias.
   - On defeat, queues a mountain reaction.


6. Add a developer console

   This is not flashy, but it may be the biggest solo-dev quality-of-life
   upgrade.

   Add commands for:

   - Teleport to authored bookmarks.
   - Toggle invulnerability.
   - Give resource.
   - Give relic.
   - Trigger level event.
   - Start mountain reaction.
   - Reload one asset or registry.
   - Print player/world/entity state.
   - Spawn encounter by ID.
   - Set cycle modifier.

   Why this matters:

   - Tuning becomes much faster.
   - Debugging becomes less painful.
   - Content can be tested in minutes instead of repeated full ascents.


Dream RPG Survival Improvements
-------------------------------

7. Build the Survival Pressure Triangle

   Cenotaph should not be a practical survival simulator, but it should have
   survival pressure. Make survival about ascent, risk, memory, and ritual.

   Add three pressure resources:

   - Vessel: health and recoverability.
   - Breath: stamina, sprint, dash, climb pressure.
   - Ash: carried unsecured memory/resource.

   Then make each route attack a different point:

   - High routes threaten Breath and falling risk.
   - Deep routes threaten Vessel through hazards and ambushes.
   - Hidden routes tempt Ash through caches and stronger rewards.

   Present the triangle as one survival sentence: the Vessel can break, Breath
   can fail, and Exposed Ash can be taken. Route ambience, HUD pressure, death
   Remembrance, and Anchor rites should all agree about which point is at risk.

   Avoid hunger, thirst, sleep, market supplies, and practical scavenging unless
   they are transformed into Cenotaph-specific rituals.


8. Make Anchors deeper ritual decisions

   The current Anchor rite is a strong foundation. Expand it carefully.

   Possible future rites:

   - Bind the Cinders: bank carried Ash and claim respawn.
   - Mend the Vessel: spend Bound Ash for healing.
   - Attune a Relic: bind one relic to gain a bonus and a curse.
   - Silence a Debt: remove one curse at a cost.
   - Wake the Route: open a dangerous shortcut or hidden path.
   - Refuse the Claim: leave unchanged, but preserve a rare condition.

   Each Anchor should feel like a negotiation with the mountain, not a menu.
   Every rite needs a visible cost, a readable mechanical result, a distinct
   mountain answer, and a memory consequence. Do not turn rite choices into a
   hidden good/evil meter; they reveal habits of preservation, obligation,
   refusal, authority, and sacrifice over time.


9. Add behavior-changing relics before adding many relics

   The game needs fewer relics with stronger identity, not a pile of stat sticks.

   Add relic effects such as:

   - Shots curve toward wounded enemies but cost stamina.
   - Killing an enemy refunds dash cooldown, but missing increases recoil.
   - Damage rises while carrying unsecured Ash.
   - Standing near an Anchor changes the weapon mode.
   - Shots mark enemies; marked enemies drop more Ash if killed quickly.
   - Low health changes projectile behavior.
   - A relic grows stronger each cycle until it betrays a defensive stat.

   Every meaningful relic should answer:

   - Whose memory or failed purpose survived inside it?
   - What useful behavior does that memory produce?
   - What does carrying it tempt the Pilgrim to repeat?
   - What condition, curse, or debt keeps it from becoming clean power?
   - How can a later Cycle remember its use differently?

   Success test:

   - A player changes route, range, movement, or target priority because of a
     relic.


10. Add the Reliquary inventory and comparison UI

   Buildcraft needs readable decisions.

   Add:

   - Compact `Reliquary` screen title with plain inventory operations.
   - Equipped relic panel.
   - Compare current relic versus pickup.
   - Show rarity, family, damage, range, cooldown, stagger, curse, and trait.
   - Preserve named relic presentation.
   - Avoid tactical stash or market aesthetics.

   The UI should feel like sorting through dangerous memories, not selling gear.
   Pair mechanical comparison with one short authored memory or burden line;
   never let flavor hide the actual tradeoff.


11. Add relic affixes and trait packages

   Relics should have identity layers.

   Suggested structure:

   - Family: Sovereign, Moonchild, Schizoid, Omission, etc.
   - Base archetype: rifle, splinter, bell, engine, crown-piece.
   - Rarity.
   - Prefix.
   - Suffix.
   - Trait package.
   - Curse or drawback.
   - Flavor memory.

   Prefixes, suffixes, traits, and curses should describe how a memory behaves,
   not assemble random gothic adjectives. Build this data-driven, with thematic
   compatibility rules, validation, and probability previews.


12. Add the first perk choice

   Do not wait for a huge skill tree. Add one meaningful choice after the first
   Anchor or in the Sanctuary prototype.

   Candidate paths:

   - Ascension: movement, height, recoil mastery, route risk, and the temptation
     to place oneself above consequence.
   - Memory: sustain, recovery, discovery, banking, witness, and obligation.
   - Omission: forbidden power, curse conversion, suppression tradeoffs, and the
     temptation to erase a cost instead of answering it.

   First useful version:

   - Pick one of three clearly named rites after resolving the First Anchor.
   - Each perk meaningfully changes combat or survival.
   - Each perk can interact with at least one relic.

   These paths are not morality colors. Any path may help or hinder atonement
   depending on what the player does with its power.


13. Add status effects that change decisions

   Start with only one or two. Avoid a generic elemental checklist.

   Good early statuses:

   - Ashen: target drops more Ash but becomes more aggressive.
   - Silence: disables one player advantage or enemy behavior temporarily.
   - Fracture: target takes more stagger but becomes harder to kill directly.

   Status effects should create counterplay, not just damage over time. Give
   each one a stable symbol, source family, HUD token, world tell, and authored
   interaction with at least one enemy, relic, hazard, or rite.


14. Add enemy roles that attack player options

   Current enemies can chase, flank, range, and attack. The next leap is enemies
   that change how the player must think.

   Add one at a time:

   - Censer: priority glass cannon that pressures stillness.
   - Harpy: vertical threat that punishes exposed high routes.
   - Chainrunner: flanker that punishes tunnel vision.
   - Silencer: suppresses relic/perk effects in a readable radius.
   - Paranoiac: escalates an encounter if not stopped.

   Success test:

   - The player says "I need to deal with that one first."
   - The encounter also communicates what failed kingdom-system, abandoned
     obligation, or distorted memory made that role persist.


15. Add enemy modifiers and elites

   Modifiers should change behavior, not just stats.

   Examples:

   - Bellwrought: announces attacks and empowers nearby enemies.
   - Ash-Buried: begins dormant, wakes if the player carries too much Ash.
   - Mirror-Touched: copies the player's last movement burst.
   - Crown-Sealed: protected until a nearby rite object is broken.
   - Starved: weaker health, faster aggression, better drops.

   Modifier names and rules should come from one primary symbol and a compatible
   Stratum. Reserve Mirror-Touched recognition logic for late-game content; do
   not scatter every motif through Ash-Walk.


16. Add a real hazard system

   Hurtbox props are a good prototype. Graduate hazards into typed data.

   Hazard types:

   - Ash storm: visibility, enemy detection, route reveal/hide.
   - Falling ruin: delayed impact and route change.
   - Chain tremor: knocks loose platforms or changes path safety.
   - Silence field: disables relic traits or HUD certainty.
   - Wind shear: affects jump, dash, projectiles, and exposure.

   Hazards should interact with route choice and enemies. Each hazard also needs
   an environmental cause the player can infer: weather, failed machinery,
   collapsing habitation, ritual suppression, or reality fracture. A hazard is
   part of the mountain's history, not a damage volume with themed particles.


17. Add route identity

   Every Stratum should eventually have route categories:

   - High Route: exposed, vertical, dangerous, better relic odds.
   - Mid Route: readable, stable, lower reward.
   - Deep Route: oppressive, secret-rich, resource pressure.

   Add route tags to level data so enemies, hazards, loot, and cycle mutations
   can bias by route. Give the categories narrative texture without turning them
   into alignment choices:

   - High routes expose authority, scale, wind, and visible risk.
   - Mid routes follow processional order and the kingdom's intended passage.
   - Deep routes uncover buried witnesses, debts, and consequences.


18. Add the first Sanctuary prototype

   Sanctuary is not just a menu hub. It is the relief point that makes survival
   pressure meaningful.

   First version:

   - Safe room after Ash-Walk.
   - Inventory and relic comparison.
   - Perk choice altar.
   - Save slot/profile surface.
   - One impossible NPC or inscription.
   - Cycle reset/continue choice.

   It should feel quiet, mournful, and useful, but never like home. Present
   inventory, perks, recovery, and Cycle choices as reliquary, attunement,
   tending, and remembrance rites. NPCs offer testimony or contradiction, not
   cheerful service counters. Plain commands and prices remain visible.


19. Add the Cycle Director spine

   Replayability should come from recombination.

   First version:

   - Generate a Run Contract at ascent start.
   - Pick three differences:

       what the player finds
       what threatens the player
       what the mountain does

   Example contract:

   - Cycle 3
   - Route bias: High Ash-Walk
   - Threat bias: Harpies earlier
   - Relic bias: Sovereign
   - Mountain condition: Ash storms reveal hidden caches
   - Memory: Warden death reaction lasts longer

   Every contract difference should have a remembered cause: a prior choice,
   an unresolved obligation, a world scar, or the mountain repeating an event
   incorrectly. Pure random variation may select among authored causes, but the
   player-facing result should never read like a detached mutator list.


20. Add persistent mountain memory

   The mountain should remember incorrectly.

   Save:

   - Defeated named enemies.
   - Bound Anchors.
   - Relics acquired.
   - Repeated deaths in a route.
   - Refused Anchor claims.
   - Secrets discovered.

   Then use that memory to alter:

   - Dialogue.
   - Encounter variants.
   - Route hazards.
   - Relic flavor.
   - Sanctuary details.
   - Mountain reactions.

   Organize memory by meaning as well as raw facts:

   - Witnessed consequence.
   - Accepted or refused obligation.
   - Authority claimed or relinquished.
   - Person, place, or relic remembered incorrectly.
   - Route escaped, endured, or returned to.

   This gives Cycle variation and final atonement the same thematic source.


21. Add event graph tracing and debug previews

   Dream logic must still have design logic. Events should be inspectable.

   Add tooling to show:

   - Which event fired.
   - Which flags were required.
   - Which actions executed.
   - Which mountain reaction queued.
   - Which save journal entries changed.
   - Why an event did not fire.


22. Add loot-table analysis tools

   Buildcraft dies if loot is unreadable or unfair.

   Add:

   - Probability preview.
   - Seeded roll preview.
   - Drop source report.
   - Duplicate/chase item checks.
   - Unreachable relic detection.
   - Route and cycle bias preview.


23. Add navmesh/pathing support

   Baseline chase is fine for now. For richer survival combat, enemies need to
   understand routes.

   Add:

   - Navmesh import or generation.
   - Path validation.
   - Blocked path diagnostics.
   - Patrol previews.
   - Runtime reload.
   - Enemy debug state.

   Do this before complex enemy behavior becomes hard to debug.


24. Add production-facing collision proxies and culling

   Before maps grow, separate what the player sees from what physics collides
   against.

   Add:

   - Render mesh asset.
   - Collision proxy asset.
   - Triangle budget validation for both.
   - Frustum culling.
   - Distance culling.
   - Measured LOD thresholds.


25. Add settings, accessibility, and failure UI

   Player-facing completeness matters even in a dark game.

   Add:

   - Mouse sensitivity.
   - Controls remapping UI.
   - Audio volume.
   - Graphics presets.
   - Text scale.
   - Reduced motion and camera shake controls.
   - Hold/toggle options.
   - Color-independent feedback.
   - Save corruption/recovery messaging.
   - Loading and rejected-content screens.


26. Add structured logs and crash reports

   A forever project needs evidence when things break.

   Add:

   - Build ID.
   - Active level.
   - Current cycle.
   - Graphics adapter.
   - Recent gameplay events.
   - Content hashes.
   - Save health.
   - Last reload result.


27. Add release packaging and content manifests

   Eventually, the runtime should know exactly what content belongs in a build.

   Add:

   - Clean package command.
   - Runtime content manifest.
   - Checksums.
   - Rejection of source-only files.
   - Schema version summary.
   - Build configuration tiers: development, playtest, profiling, release.


Level Editor Requirement
------------------------

Cenotaph needs a dedicated level editor. This should become one of the most
important solo-development tools in the project.

The editor should not be a freeform content generator. It should be a controlled
authoring surface for the existing data contracts:

  levels/*.json
  prefabs/*.json
  data/enemies/*.toml
  data/relics/*.toml
  config/*.toml

The editor's job is to help build ascents quickly while preserving validation,
stable IDs, ritual language, and the mountain's identity.

The editor itself should remain a quiet professional tool. Do not bury transform
fields, IDs, errors, or simulation controls under diegetic labels. Theme belongs
in authored metadata and previews, not in making development slower.


28. Build a Cenotaph level editor

   Core principle:

   - The editor edits authored data.
   - The game runtime consumes authored data.
   - Validation remains the gate between them.
   - No generated content should silently enter runtime folders.

   The editor should support:

   - Base map selection.
   - Prop placement.
   - Stable prop IDs.
   - Asset preview.
   - Collider selection.
   - Enemy placement.
   - Enemy definition assignment.
   - Enemy health override.
   - Enemy loot table assignment.
   - Enemy defeat event hook.
   - Anchor placement and Anchor event hook.
   - Loot pickup placement.
   - Loot table editing.
   - Enemy path editing.
   - Moving platform/path editing.
   - Event trigger editing.
   - Event action editing.
   - Dialogue editing.
   - Mountain reaction editing.
   - Ambient particle profile editing.
   - Ambient sound profile editing.
   - Prop ambient sound placement.
   - Surface material and texture assignment.
   - Route tags and route preview.
   - Home Stratum and allowed-Cycle mutation tags.
   - Narrative role: consequence, witness, temptation, obligation, recognition,
     judgment, or release.
   - Primary recurring symbol and spoiler tier.
   - Emotional beat: shelter, approach, threat, choice, recovery, revelation,
     or rite.
   - Semantic HUD, ambience, particle, mountain-answer, and memory hooks.
   - Prefab placement and prefab editing.
   - Validation report panel.
   - Play-from-here / reload-current-level workflow.


29. Level editor MVP: Data-first 2D/3D authoring

   First version should be practical, not beautiful.

   Minimum features:

   - Open a level JSON file.
   - Show base map and prop positions.
   - Select, move, rotate, and scale props.

   cold Anchor light. It should be beautiful, restrained, and readable.

   Add:

   - A more polished player status cluster.
   - Better Vessel, Breath, dash, Exposed Ash, and Bound Ash presentation.
   - A prettier reticle with stateful hit, kill, block, and miss responses.

Full Codebase Audit Additions
-----------------------------

Audit snapshot: 2026-07-18

The complete roadmap, current design documents, Rust runtime, shaders, authored
levels, enemy/relic definitions, configuration, save system, validation tools,
tests, asset rules, and supplied visual references were reviewed together.

The foundation is healthier than an ordinary prototype:

- 184 automated tests pass.
- All three levels, the prefab, configs, enemy definitions, relic definitions,
  models, and textures pass content validation.
- The project doctor reports no warnings.
- Transactional content reload, staged saves, backup recovery, deterministic
  authored loot, named encounter presentation, mountain reactions, and strict
  validation are already unusually strong foundations.

The audit also found several important contradictions between authored intent
and runtime behavior:

- Most fundamentally, the build has no explicit player-facing goal, reachable
  victory state, completion presentation, credits, or campaign ending contract.
  Ash-Walk's return gate loops directly to the foundation test map without
  checking that the Warden, relic reward, or First Anchor arc is complete.
- Death advances the Cycle, but it does not rebuild or repopulate the ascent.
- The save journal describes only the currently loaded level, so returning to a
  previous level cannot preserve a whole multi-level pilgrimage coherently.
- Hurtbox damage is multiplied by one frame's delta time and then throttled by a
  longer cooldown, making authored damage-per-second depend on frame rate and
  land far below its configured value.
- The safety floor catches falls just below the map; there is no authored void,
  fall damage, kill volume, or recovery rule for a game about vertical danger.
- `is_climbable`, `ambient_sound_id`, prop `dialogue_id`, `LevelPathKind`,
  `crit_multiplier`, several lighting/fog tuning values, and prop light
  intensity are authored or validated without complete runtime behavior.
- Prop light color currently affects material tint; it is not a local light.
- Harpy behavior is still horizontal chase behavior with a speed multiplier;
  paths also ignore authored vertical movement and path kind.
- Enemy attacks have no perception, line-of-sight, hearing, home, leash, or
  world-occlusion contract.
- Combat is one hitscan sphere test with no typed damage result, hit regions,
  projectile behavior, weapon action model, or real critical-hit path.
- Duplicate relic pickups report reinforcement, but no reinforcement mechanic
  exists.
- Engine state and frame logic remain concentrated in very large modules, while
  runtime enemy state is aligned to mutable prop-vector indexes.
- The renderer uses one camera-following light and a simple diffuse material;
  authored point lights, shadows, local fog, backdrop scale, and material roles
  do not yet exist.
- The HUD reserves objective and status-effect regions, but the frame contract
  has no objective or status-effect data yet.
- Audio correctly keeps one-shot hooks silent, but it has no authored recording
  registry, mixer buses, spatial emitters, occlusion, or zone runtime.
- `tools/level_editor/` exists but is empty.

These are not reasons to distrust the foundation. They are the clearest map of
what should be built next.

Audit-Critical Core Corrections
-------------------------------

51. Define the canonical ascent lifecycle

   Write one explicit state machine for:

     Boot -> Sanctuary/Start -> Ascent -> Anchor Claim -> Death/Respawn
     -> Stratum Transition -> Coronation Failure -> New Cycle
     -> Final Atonement -> Credits/Echo Archive

   Decide separately:

   - What an Attempt is.
   - What an Ascent is.
   - What a Cycle is.
   - Which event advances each one.
   - Which enemies, pickups, events, and hazards reset at death.
   - Which reset only at a new ascent or Cycle.
   - Which changes are permanent mountain memory.

   Do not let `cycle.advance()` remain synonymous with every player death unless
   that is an intentional, tested design rule. The HUD, saves, loot seed, enemy
   scaling, and world reset must all agree on the same lifecycle.


52. Correct the damage-over-time and hazard timing contract

   Replace frame-delta damage followed by a longer cooldown with one of two
   explicit models:

   - Continuous damage: apply `damage_per_second * simulation_step` every fixed
     step while exposed.
   - Pulsed damage: apply an authored `damage_per_tick` at an authored interval.

   Add tests at 30, 60, 120, and irregular simulated frame rates. The same
   exposure duration must cause the same damage.

   Every damage source should report:

   - Stable source ID.
   - Damage kind.
   - Raw and final amount.
   - Hit direction.
   - Whether it staggered, killed, was blocked, or was resisted.


53. Add a real falling, void, and out-of-bounds system

   Vertical danger needs rules, not an invisible safety floor.

   Add:

   - Authored kill or recovery volumes.
   - A configurable world-fall threshold.
   - Fall-distance or impact-speed tracking.
   - Fall damage with readable thresholds.
   - Ledge recovery only where the design permits it.
   - A safe respawn/rewind rule when the player leaves valid world bounds.
   - A debug-only safety floor toggle instead of a universal gameplay floor.
   - Editor visualization and validation for recovery volumes and safe spawns.

   A fall should be a route consequence. It should never become an endless fall,
   a hidden floor walk, or a soft lock beneath the level.


54. Make vertical traversal worthy of the premise

   `is_climbable` currently has no runtime behavior. Either implement it or
   remove it until the contract is ready.

   A conservative Cenotaph traversal set:

   - Reliable step-up and slope handling.
   - Jump buffering and existing coyote time.
   - Authored mantle points or mantleable height ranges.
   - Short ledge grabs where explicitly tagged.
   - Stamina pressure for sustained authored climbs.
   - Moving-platform carry behavior.
   - Wind and suppression modifiers that alter known traversal rules.

   Avoid trendy wall-running and unlimited climbing. Traversal should make
   height dangerous, deliberate, and route-defining.


55. Perform a schema-to-runtime parity pass

   Create a generated coverage report for every long-lived authored field:

     Authored -> Parsed -> Validated -> Executed -> Observable -> Tested

   Immediately resolve the current partial fields:

   - `PropData.is_climbable`.
   - `PropData.ambient_sound_id`.
   - `PropData.dialogue_id`.
   - `PropData.light_color` and `light_intensity`.
   - `LevelPathData.kind` and vertical waypoints.
   - `CombatConfig.crit_multiplier`.
   - World fog and lighting tuning values superseded by level atmosphere.
   - Asset imports that are authoring metadata rather than runtime data.

   Mark editor-only metadata explicitly. Warn on deprecated fields. Reject
   gameplay fields that silently do nothing.


56. Persist an entire ascent, not only the current level

   Replace the single current-level snapshot with a journal keyed by stable
   Stratum/level ID.

   Store per level:

   - Fired events and flags.
   - Defeated or collected authored entity IDs.
   - Loose generated loot.
   - Encounter state when persistence is intended.
   - Opened shortcuts and changed routes.
   - Pending mountain answers.

   Store separately:

   - Current attempt state.
   - Current ascent state.
   - Between-Cycle progression.
   - Permanent mountain memory.
   - Witnessed consequences, completed obligations, identity evidence, and
     other author-approved atonement state.

   Transitioning away and returning must not revive or erase content by accident.
   New-ascent and new-Cycle reset policies should clear only their owned layer.


57. Add causal gameplay commands and an event ledger

   Introduce typed commands/events with a stable cause chain:

     EnemyDefeated(source)
       -> EncounterProgressed
       -> LootManifested
       -> LevelEventRequested
       -> MountainReactionQueued
       -> SaveTransactionCommitted

   Add:

   - Event instance ID, source entity ID, simulation tick, and cause ID.
   - Ordered actions with explicit failure policy.
   - A bounded event history for HUD, audio, logs, tests, and console tools.
   - Atomic commit groups for enemy defeat/loot/event and Anchor claim/reaction.
   - Idempotency keys so retrying a transaction cannot duplicate rewards.
   - Distinct event instances so the same reusable mountain-reaction profile can
     be queued twice for two different causes.


58. Extract shared content and simulation foundations

   The level editor, headless tests, validators, and game should share code
   without pulling in a GPU window.

   Evolve toward a small Cargo workspace or clean internal library boundaries:

   - `cenotaph_content`: schemas, typed IDs, migrations, registries, validation.
   - `cenotaph_sim`: runtime world, fixed-step rules, events, combat, saves.
   - `cenotaph_game`: winit/wgpu/Rapier/rodio application and presentation.
   - `cenotaph_editor`: authoring UI using the same content crate.
   - `cenotaph_tools`: doctor, simulations, packaging, reports.

   This does not require an ECS rewrite. It requires ownership boundaries and a
   headless simulation surface.


59. Add an explicit application and screen-state machine

   `Playing` and `Paused` are not enough for a complete RPG.

   Add states for:

   - Boot and compatibility check.
   - Profile/save selection.
   - Loading and transition.
   - Playing.
   - Pause/options.
   - Inventory and item comparison.
   - Anchor rite.
   - Dialogue/conversation.
   - Death and respawn.
   - Sanctuary.
   - Test-pilgrimage completion.
   - Coronation failure and Cycle transition.
   - Final judgment, release, credits, and remembered-Echo selection.
   - Recoverable and fatal errors.

   Each state must own allowed input, cursor behavior, simulation pause policy,
   audio snapshot, HUD layers, and save permissions.


60. Add deterministic input commands, seed accounting, and replay

   Sample device input once, convert it into per-tick gameplay commands, and let
   fixed-step simulation consume those commands.

   Record:

   - Build/content hash.
   - Initial save snapshot.
   - Run-contract seed and all derived seeds.
   - Tick-stamped player commands.
   - Important nondeterministic external events.

   Use replay for movement bugs, combat fairness, save reconstruction, editor
   playtests, and performance comparisons. A seed ledger should explain which
   subsystem derived each random result instead of relying on hidden hash calls.


Combat, Enemy, Loot, and Build Depth
------------------------------------

61. Build a typed combat resolution pipeline

   Replace direct health subtraction with a testable resolver:

     AttackIntent -> TargetQuery -> HitResult -> DamagePacket
     -> Mitigation/Traits -> Health/Stagger/Status -> CombatEvents

   Support:

   - Damage source and owner.
   - Damage kind and tags.
   - Weak point or hit region.
   - Critical rules.
   - Stagger/poise pressure.
   - Armor, ward, silence, and resistance hooks.
   - Friendly/self/world damage policy.
   - Final result with explainable modifiers.

   Wire or remove the currently unused critical multiplier. Keep formulas small
   enough that a player can understand why an attack worked. Damage tags should
   come from an authored source and semantic rule, not a generic element wheel.
   The same result must drive Vessel feedback, relic memory, enemy response, and
   Remembrance consistently.


62. Create a data-driven weapon action architecture

   Relics currently multiply one universal hitscan attack. Add a weapon/action
   definition that can express meaningful families without hard-coded branches.

   Candidate fields:

   - Action kind: hitscan, projectile, burst, charged, placed, or pulse.
   - Fire cadence and recovery.
   - Range, spread, recoil, impact, stagger, and falloff.
   - Cost model: cooldown, heat, charge, Ash, Breath, or authored ammunition.
   - Alternate action or conditional mode.
   - Projectile and impact profile IDs.
   - Trait hooks and semantic sound/VFX hooks.
   - Memory source, ritual use, temptation, burden, and home-Stratum tags.

   Decide the ammo/heat/cooldown question deliberately. Do not inherit practical
   military reload simulation by default. A relic's action and cost should feel
   like the behavior of a surviving memory, not a gun with a poetic skin.


63. Add projectile, surface, and impact contracts

   Some relic families and enemies need space-aware attacks.

   Add:

   - Pooled projectile entities with lifetime, owner, collision groups, and save
     policy.
   - Swept collision or CCD for fast projectiles and dashes.
   - Surface material tags such as stone, iron, ash, flesh, Anchor, and void.
   - Impact result hooks for damage, stagger, decals, particles, and authored
     sound.
   - Area and line attacks with visible warning geometry.
   - Wind influence only for attacks authored to receive it.

   Every projectile must have a readable lifetime, threat direction, and cleanup
   rule. Surface responses should reinforce material meaning: Anchor, ash, iron,
   mirror, and void cannot all answer impacts with the same spark recolored.


64. Add a relic trait/effect runtime

   Behavior-changing relics need more than additional TOML multipliers.

   Build typed effect hooks such as:

   - Before attack.
   - After hit, miss, kill, dash, damage, Anchor rite, pickup, or route entry.
   - While low health, airborne, carrying Ash, near an Anchor, or suppressed.
   - On status applied or removed.

   Effects should emit commands through the same gameplay event pipeline, obey
   stacking rules, expose their active condition to the HUD, and remain
   deterministic in tests. Every trait needs an authored memory premise, useful
   temptation, visible condition, and consequence that can be remembered.


65. Save relic instances, provenance, and rolled identity

   Definition IDs are sufficient for the current four fixed relics, but generated
   loot needs stable instances.

   A relic instance should remember:

   - Stable instance ID.
   - Base definition and family.
   - Rarity, affixes, trait package, and curse.
   - Rolled values and generation version.
   - Source level, route, enemy/event, Cycle, and seed.
   - Attunement, reinforcement, mutation, and equipped state.
   - Authored memory variant and any contradiction learned in later Cycles.

   Content migrations must preserve shipped relic identity or explicitly retire
   it with player-facing recovery. Provenance is not hidden metadata only; it is
   how the Reliquary shows that a relic came from a particular witness, keeper,
   route, or failed obligation.


66. Resolve physical loot, pickup choice, and duplicate behavior

   Current generic enemy rewards can enter inventory immediately, while authored
   enemy loot manifests in the world. Unify the player-facing rule.

   Add:

   - Physical, source-stable drops for meaningful relics.
   - Clear inspect/claim/leave behavior when a decision matters.
   - Automatic pickup only for simple Ash or deliberately trivial resources.
   - Drop protection against inaccessible geometry and voids.
   - A nearby safe manifestation point fallback.
   - A real duplicate outcome: reinforce, remember, convert, mutate, or reject.
   - No message claiming reinforcement until a reinforcement mechanic exists.

   A duplicate should be framed as the mountain returning the same memory
   differently. The mechanical outcome must remain explicit and deterministic;
   theme cannot conceal whether an item was strengthened, transformed, or lost.


67. Define progression layers and resource economy

   Bound Ash needs meaningful sinks or it will only accumulate.

   Define:

   - Temporary in-attempt power.
   - In-ascent build state.
   - Between-Cycle unlock breadth.
   - Permanent mountain memory.

   Give Bound Ash a small set of ritual uses:

   - Mend the Vessel.
   - Attune or unbind a relic.
   - Wake a shortcut.
   - Accept a perk rite.
   - Pay a memory debt.

   Simulate sources and sinks across many seeded ascents. Avoid vendors,
   commodities, repair chores, and an economy that rewards grinding weak rooms.
   Ash is memory and obligation, not gold with another name. Every major sink
   should be a rite that preserves, changes, surrenders, or answers something.


68. Decide what death leaves behind

   Losing all unsecured Ash is clear, but a dream survival RPG can make the loss
   more expressive.

   Evaluate one Cenotaph-aligned rule:

   - The mountain keeps all exposed Ash.
   - A recoverable Ash Echo appears once on the failed route.
   - The next Anchor offers a debt to reclaim part of it.
   - A relic or perk can alter the loss at a real cost.

   Whichever rule wins must have anti-exploit behavior, unreachable-drop
   recovery, save safety, clear HUD stakes, and consistent Cycle semantics. The
   death Remembrance should state the exact loss first, then show how the
   mountain interprets or misremembers it.


69. Add stat provenance and a build inspector

   Every final stat should be explainable as:

     Base + additive sources -> multiplicative sources -> caps -> final value

   Add a developer and player-facing breakdown for:

   - Damage and cadence.
   - Range, recoil, spread, and stagger.
   - Health, Breath, movement, and dash.
   - Loot/route biases.
   - Status resistance and curse pressure.

   Detect invalid stacking, dead traits, circular effects, unreachable caps, and
   upgrades that never change a decision. Present the player-facing version as
   `Attunement`: base Vessel/Breath and relic action remain readable, while every
   rite, curse, memory, and Cycle source can be inspected.


70. Add fair enemy perception

   Enemies need to perceive the player before they attack.

   Add:

   - Sight cone and distance.
   - World line-of-sight raycasts.
   - Optional hearing events for loud actions.
   - Alert, investigate, combat, search, and return-home states.
   - Home position and leash rules.
   - Shared alert policy by encounter rather than global awareness.
   - Debug perception probes and last-known-position display.

   A Censer must not damage through a wall merely because horizontal distance is
   inside its attack range.


71. Make enemy movement genuinely three-dimensional

   Enforce `LevelPathKind` and support movement appropriate to each kind:

   - Enemy patrol paths.
   - NPC paths.
   - Kinematic platform paths that carry riders.
   - Cinematic/debug paths that never become gameplay movers accidentally.

   For aerial roles add perch points, altitude bands, dive arcs, recovery arcs,
   and reachable cover checks. A Harpy must threaten vertical space, not simply
   run faster across the ground plane.


72. Give attacks readable states and recovery

   Build a reusable enemy action state machine:

     Select -> Telegraph -> Commit -> Active -> Recover -> Cooldown

   Add:

   - Range and line-of-sight revalidation before commit.
   - Interrupt and stagger windows.
   - Turn-rate limits during committed attacks.
   - Readable world and HUD tells.
   - Semantic animation, particle, and authored sound hooks.
   - Deterministic tests for dodge windows and damage timing.

   Difficulty should come from layered decisions, not invisible instant damage.
   Telegraph presentation should inherit the attack's source symbol and enemy
   role. Reserve generic red danger treatment for immediate bodily harm rather
   than making every mechanic look identical.


73. Add group coordination and encounter reset rules

   Prevent every active melee enemy from occupying the same point or attacking
   simultaneously.

   Add:

   - Attack-token or pressure-slot budgets.
   - Desired spacing and local avoidance.
   - Role-aware positioning.
   - Spawn safety and off-camera appearance rules.
   - Encounter bounds and retreat behavior.
   - Reset, partial reset, or persistence policy after player death/escape.
   - Anti-stuck recovery with debug evidence.


74. Build a reusable named encounter and boss framework

   Extend encounter definitions into runtime ownership:

   - Activation and arena bounds.
   - Participants, reinforcements, phases, and completion rules.
   - Gates, hazards, and objective links.
   - Named HUD identity and phase messaging.
   - Retry/reset policy.
   - Guaranteed reward transaction.
   - Mountain answer and memory consequence.
   - Save/resume policy at every phase boundary.
   - Player question, symbolic role, home Stratum, and spoiler tier.
   - Why its completion changes the mountain instead of merely clearing a room.

   Use the Ash-Warden as the first named-encounter test, not as a reason to build
   a giant boss system immediately. Player-facing panels should lead with its
   authored name and title; `boss` and `encounter` remain internal tool terms.


World, Ascent, and Narrative Structure
--------------------------------------

75. Add a Stratum and route topology graph

   A level file describes objects, but the ascent also needs explicit topology.

   Model:

   - Strata and sub-areas.
   - Route nodes and connections.
   - Altitude and route category.
   - Anchors, Sanctuary links, shortcuts, gates, and return paths.
   - Encounter, hazard, reward, objective, and ambience ownership.
   - Conditions that open, close, replace, or distort an edge.
   - Narrative beat, primary symbol, and spoiler tier for every major node.

   This graph should drive route objectives, Cycle variation, save memory,
   editor visualization, and Remembrance. It should also make the canonical arc
   from physical ruin to judgment visible to authors without exposing it to the
   player as a spoiler-filled campaign map.


76. Add authored modular route assembly

   Prefer authored modules with controlled recombination over fully procedural
   terrain.

   Add reusable route pieces with:

   - Entry/exit sockets.
   - Bounds and collision proxies.
   - Altitude gain and route tags.
   - Encounter/hazard/loot sockets.
   - Landmark and sightline metadata.
   - Allowed neighbors and repetition limits.
   - Deterministic seed preview.
   - Theme socket: home Stratum, emotional beat, primary symbol, and allowed
     narrative roles.

   Validate reachability, traversal difficulty, pacing, duplicate modules, and
   safe Anchor placement before a generated layout can ship. Recombination must
   not produce a mechanically valid but thematically incoherent route.


77. Make shortcuts and world changes first-class

   Add typed world-state changes instead of relying only on removed props and
   flags:

   - Open/close gate.
   - Enable/disable collision or hazard.
   - Change path or route edge.
   - Move or replace an authored entity.
   - Reveal/bury a cache.
   - Change an Anchor or Sanctuary station.
   - Leave a named world scar.

   Each change needs a stable ID, reversible/irreversible policy, save layer,
   editor preview, and migration behavior. It also needs an authored cause and
   answer so a `world scar` means the mountain remembered a consequence rather
   than a flag silently toggled geometry.


78. Add a discovery, secret, and memory-chain system

   Secrets should be authored conditions with clues, not random hidden pickups.

   Support:

   - Discovery IDs and states: unknown, hinted, witnessed, understood,
     contradicted, remembered.
   - Route, relic, event, Cycle, death-count, and prior-memory conditions.
   - Multi-step chains across Strata.
   - Optional rewards that are mechanical, narrative, or both.
   - No marker until the secret has earned one.
   - Debug explanation for why a secret can or cannot appear.

   Classify chains as witness, obligation, identity, authority, relinquishment,
   contradiction, or world scar. These categories feed atonement without
   exposing a collectible checklist or a completion percentage.


79. Expand the event language without creating a scripting trap

   Current events have a useful small action set. Add capability in typed,
   testable increments.

   Needed conditions:

   - Boolean all/any/not groups.
   - Flag, objective, encounter, route, Cycle, relic, resource, and memory tests.

   Needed actions:

   - Start/complete/fail objective.
   - Start/reset/complete encounter.
   - Enable/disable/transform authored entity.
   - Change route or shortcut state.
   - Start/stop ambience or particle zone.
   - Apply status or perk choice.
   - Delay, sequence, branch, or wait for a condition.

   Keep actions serializable, deterministic, traceable, and transactional. Avoid
   embedding an unrestricted scripting language until typed actions truly fail.


80. Add conditional dialogue and conversation state

   The current timed line list is a good inscription system. Build conversations
   separately when NPCs arrive.

   Add:

   - Stable localization/text IDs.
   - Conditions and authored variants.
   - Player responses only where they create a real ritual or knowledge choice.
   - Actions and memory changes after a response.
   - Interrupt/resume policy.
   - Cycle-specific contradiction and NPC misremembering.
   - Conversation history tests.

   Preserve uncertainty. Do not turn every NPC into a complete quest explainer.
   A conversation should reveal a desire, testimony, contradiction, or debt;
   avoid dialogue that exists only to deliver a task marker or lore paragraph.


81. Add a remembrance archive

   Give discovered lore, relic memories, named encounters, Anchors, symbols, and
   contradictions a quiet home in Sanctuary.

   The archive should:

   - Record what the player actually witnessed.
   - Preserve conflicting versions instead of resolving them automatically.
   - Link a relic to its remembered source and mutations.
   - Hint at incomplete chains without exposing exact checklist solutions.
   - Survive content migrations safely.
   - Avoid becoming a generic completion-percentage encyclopedia.

   Separate witnessed fact from the mountain's interpretation. Mechanical
   records remain trustworthy; contradictory testimony remains visibly sourced
   so mystery feels intentional rather than like database corruption.


82. Build landmark, vista, and vertical composition support

   The supplied mountain concept depends on readable scale far beyond the active
   combat space.

   Add solo-friendly presentation tools for:

   - Distant mountain/backdrop layers.
   - Low-cost silhouettes and impostors.
   - Altitude-aware fog and cloud bands.
   - Persistent summit/Anchor/Stratum landmarks.
   - Occlusion corridors that reveal scale in stages.
   - Editor sightline and landmark previews.
   - A visible sense of height gained and distance survived.

   The player should periodically see where they came from and something they
   still cannot reach.


83. Add a pressure and rest pacing model

   Survival becomes exhausting when every meter has equal intensity.

   Tag spaces as:

   - Shelter.
   - Approach.
   - Threat.
   - Recovery.
   - Choice.
   - Revelation.
   - Anchor/Sanctuary.

   Validate that routes alternate pressure and release, that recovery cannot be
   farmed infinitely, and that named encounters have readable approaches. Let
   the Cycle Director alter pressure patterns without erasing authored rhythm.
   Tag the emotional purpose too: isolation, insignificance, forgotten life,
   temptation, recognition, judgment, or uncertain mercy.


84. Teach through the pilgrimage itself

   Add a diegetic onboarding contract for movement, combat, Ash risk, relic
   choice, Anchor rites, objectives, and route choice.

   Use:

   - Safe opportunities before required tests.
   - Context prompts that disappear after demonstrated mastery.
   - Oath stones, waystones, environment framing, and consequences.
   - Optional reminder access in pause/settings.
   - Telemetry that reveals where first-time players fail to understand a rule.

   Do not fill Ash-Walk with tutorial panels or lore-breaking instruction text.


Presentation, Audio, Input, and Accessibility Additions
------------------------------------------------------

85. Upgrade rendering through a measured stylized path

   Do not jump directly to an expensive full-PBR renderer. Add the visual tools
   that most support the concept art and combat readability:

   - Stable tone mapping and exposure.
   - Directional key light plus a small budget of authored local lights.
   - One tested shadow solution with quality tiers.
   - Height/local fog volumes.
   - Stylized roughness/metal/emissive material roles.
   - Normal maps only when supplied assets benefit from them.
   - Sky/backdrop and cloud-band support.
   - Restrained post effects for damage, silence, memory, and mountain answers.

   Every feature needs before/after captures, GPU timing, and a fallback tier.
   Grade each Stratum around its reserved emotional and material identity rather
   than applying one sepia, blue, or ash filter to the whole mountain. Semantic
   colors must still mean the same gameplay state in every grade.


86. Make authored prop lights real

   `light_color` and `light_intensity` should either drive a local light or be
   renamed as emissive tint.

   A real local-light system needs:

   - Point/spot type.
   - Color, intensity, range, and optional cone.
   - Static/dynamic flag.
   - Priority and per-view budget.
   - Optional event/flag/Cycle condition.
   - Editor radius and cost preview.
   - Clustered/tiled or conservative CPU selection only when measurements demand
     it.


87. Add an authored VFX profile registry

   Current effect bursts are hard-coded by semantic enum. Move shape-neutral
   behavior into validated profiles without inventing production visuals.

   Profiles can own:

   - Lifetime, count, size, velocity, drag, gravity, and fade.
   - Developer-approved color/material references.
   - Surface and status variants.
   - Budget class and pooling policy.
   - Semantic hooks for attacks, impacts, pickups, rites, and reactions.
   - Primary symbol, home Stratum, emotional role, and spoiler tier.

   Keep neutral placeholders until visual direction exists. The tool should make
   authored art easy to connect later, not generate art direction itself. Theme
   validation should reject reward confetti, motif soup, and one recolored burst
   reused for unrelated rites, damage, loot, and judgment.


88. Add animation and presentation state hooks

   Gameplay already has enough states to expose a real presentation contract.

   Add semantic states for:

   - Idle, locomotion, alert, windup, active attack, recovery, stagger, death.
   - Relic idle, fire, charge, reload/vent if applicable, and inspect.
   - Anchor dormant, available, blocked, claimed, and answering.

   Support shader motion, transform animation, and later skeletal clips through
   the same state interface. Do not infer anatomy or create production animation
   without developer-supplied assets and direction.


89. Add a first-person relic presentation pipeline

   Shooter feel needs the carried relic to have presence even before final art.

   Build support for:

   - Separate view-model render layer and camera projection.
   - Authored view-model asset and attachment points.
   - Muzzle/projectile origin independent from collision ray safety.
   - Fire, recoil, charge, cooldown/heat, inspect, and swap states.
   - World-model versus carried-model references.
   - FOV, handedness, motion, and visibility accessibility options.
   - Semantic presentation for the relic's memory, burden, active condition, and
     temptation without inventing its physical form.

   Keep the view model absent or a single neutral primitive until the user
   supplies the relic's visual direction.


90. Build an authored audio asset and mixer system

   Preserve the current silence policy while making future recordings easy to
   integrate.

   Add:

   - Validated audio definitions and safe runtime paths.
   - Semantic cue IDs mapped to developer-approved recordings.
   - Mixer buses: master, ambience, world, combat, dialogue, UI.
   - Volume, mute, dynamic range, and pause snapshots.
   - Polyphony limits, priority, random-without-repeat variants, and cleanup.
   - 3D emitters, distance falloff, panning, occlusion, and interior/exterior
     sends.
   - Ambient-zone crossfades and mountain-reaction snapshots.
   - Captions for important non-speech threats and events.
   - Authored sound-role and Stratum ownership metadata.
   - Silence as a valid resolved cue when no approved recording exists.

   Do not synthesize one-shot stand-ins while assets are missing. Keep warning,
   machinery, exposure, rite, witness, suppression, judgment, and release roles
   distinct in the mix. Do not turn every important event into a bell or choir.


91. Replace the prototype glyph renderer with production text support

   The current compact ASCII glyph system is clever and testable, but it strips
   punctuation and cannot support localization or rich authored prose.

   Add:

   - Font atlas or text-rendering library with cached glyphs.
   - Unicode, punctuation, diacritics, kerning, wrapping, and fallback fonts.
   - Text style tokens and semantic emphasis.
   - Scalable UI and subtitle sizes.
   - Localization IDs with source-language fallback.
   - Layout tests using the longest supported translations.

   Preserve the current HUD's restrained geometry while letting names and
   dialogue retain their real written form. Reserve display treatments for
   authored names, rites, Strata, Coronation outcomes, and Final Release; plain
   controls, costs, errors, and accessibility labels remain plain.


92. Add complete keyboard, mouse, and gamepad ownership

   Add:

   - Rebinding UI with conflict resolution.
   - Multiple bindings per action.
   - Mouse sensitivity per axis, invert Y, raw input, and smoothing options.
   - Controller navigation, dead zones, response curves, vibration controls,
     glyph switching, and aim assistance options.
   - Hold/toggle choices for sprint, aim, interact, and crouch if added.
   - UI focus navigation in every modal and screen.
   - Input-context tests so one press cannot trigger dialogue, a rite, and a world
     event together.


93. Add difficulty and accessibility contracts, not only settings

   Define which systems can change without breaking authored encounters:

   - Enemy damage and aggression.
   - Telegraph duration.
   - Aim assistance.
   - Resource-loss mercy.
   - Navigation hint strength.
   - Pause behavior and input timing windows.

   Accessibility additions:

   - Reduced shake, bob, FOV motion, flashes, and particle density.
   - High-contrast interactables and color-independent states.
   - Subtitle/caption background and speaker labels.
   - Text scale and readable-font option.
   - Photosensitivity review.

   Avoid difficulty modes that only create health sponges.
   Optional in-world preset names may frame the mountain's pressure, but every
   preset must include a literal description of the changed rules. Accessibility
   options never require an in-world justification and never affect atonement.


Level Editor and Solo-Development Tooling Additions
--------------------------------------------------

94. Give the level editor a shared, versioned foundation

   Start `tools/level_editor/` as a real workspace member using the shared
   content crate.

   The editor must:

   - Load through official migrations.
   - Preserve unknown/new fields when possible.
   - Save deterministically with minimal unrelated diffs.
   - Use the exact runtime validators.
   - Never require launching the GPU game to inspect a data error.
   - Keep editor preferences outside authored level files.
   - Share theme-profile, Stratum-reservation, symbol, spoiler, mountain-answer,
     and Remembrance contracts with runtime and validation.

   Keep the editor's own interface functional and restrained. Its job is to make
   themed authorship inspectable, not to roleplay as a mysterious machine.


95. Add the everyday scene-editing ergonomics

   Beyond the MVP, add:

   - Undo/redo command stack.
   - Autosave and crash recovery.
   - Multi-select, duplicate, delete, group, and parent operations.
   - Translate/rotate/scale gizmos.
   - Grid, angle, surface, and socket snapping.
   - Outliner, search, filters, visibility, and lockable layers.
   - Local/world transform modes and numeric entry.
   - Bookmarks, camera speeds, orthographic views, and focus selection.
   - Copy/paste with safe local ID remapping.

   Stable dimensions and predictable transforms matter more than decorative
   editor styling.


96. Add a content reference graph and safe ID refactors

   Stable IDs are persistence contracts, so renaming must be deliberate.

   Add tools to:

   - Find every incoming and outgoing reference.
   - Rename an unshipped ID transactionally.
   - Retire a shipped ID with migration metadata.
   - Detect orphaned, unreachable, duplicate, or cyclic content.
   - Show which saves/content versions may be affected.
   - Compare authored graph changes before save.

   Never perform a blind text replacement across JSON/TOML.


97. Add prefab variants and semantic sockets

   Current prefabs cannot carry level-local graph links. Preserve that safety and
   add explicit reusable interfaces:

   - Parameterized local IDs.
   - Named sockets for encounters, loot, paths, events, lights, ambience, and
     objectives.
   - Variant sets for material, route role, and Cycle mutation.
   - Instance override tracking.
   - Apply/revert/update workflow.
   - Validation that no duplicated prefab silently shares persistence identity.


98. Add a live editor-to-game playtest bridge

   Use a small local protocol or file-watched command channel for:

   - Load current level.
   - Play from selected bookmark.
   - Select the runtime entity that corresponds to an editor object.
   - Trigger selected event, encounter, objective, reaction, or dialogue.
   - Preview the selected feature's world ownership, player decision, expected
     answer, and Remembrance result beside its raw runtime state.
   - Inspect runtime state and validation failures.
   - Return death/stuck/performance markers to the editor.

   Keep the runtime authoritative during play and the editor authoritative for
   authored content. Never silently write live mutable state back into a level.


99. Add dedicated simulation and balance laboratories

   Build headless tools for:

   - Loot distributions and pity/chase behavior.
   - Encounter time-to-kill and incoming-pressure envelopes.
   - Enemy composition budgets.
   - Relic/perk/status combinations.
   - Resource sources, sinks, death loss, and Anchor costs.
   - Run-contract variety and repetition.
   - Route reachability and expected pressure.
   - Symbol frequency, Stratum leakage, spoiler timing, and theme-profile
     coverage across generated/recombined content.

   Reports should show outliers and explanations, not declare balance or theme
   correct. A human author decides whether a rare contradiction is meaningful.


100. Add source-asset dependency and bake tooling

   Track:

   - Source asset -> runtime export -> levels/prefabs/definitions -> packaged
     build.
   - Hash, import settings, dimensions, materials, collision proxy, and budgets.
   - Stale exports and missing sources.
   - Generated versus developer-authored support assets.

   Add explicit bakes only when required: collision proxies, navmesh, occlusion,
   thumbnails, or impostors. Every bake should be reproducible and report its
   source inputs.


Testing, Performance, and Production Additions
----------------------------------------------

101. Record local playtest traces and route heatmaps

   Add an opt-in local trace containing:

   - Position and altitude sampled at a low rate.
   - Route and objective transitions.
   - Damage taken/dealt and source.
   - Death, fall, stuck, and respawn locations.
   - Relics seen, claimed, equipped, or ignored.
   - Ash carried/lost/bound over time.
   - Encounter duration and enemy priority order.
   - Event and mountain-reaction timeline.

   Import traces into the level editor as paths and heatmaps. Keep this local and
   privacy-conscious unless the player explicitly exports a report.


102. Add CPU, GPU, memory, and content budgets

   Instrument named frame phases:

   - Input and fixed-step simulation.
   - Physics.
   - AI and encounters.
   - Events and saves.
   - Instance synchronization.
   - Culling and draw submission.
   - Scene, particles, post effects, and HUD GPU passes.

Purpose, Victory, and Endgame Corrections
-----------------------------------------

These items are numbered last because they were found in the second audit pass,
not because they should be built last. Items 111-112 now belong at the front of
the implementation order.

111. Define the canonical player goal and victory contract

   Write one short, literal answer for each of these questions:

   - Why does the player leave safety and climb?
   - What must the player accomplish in the current Stratum?
   - What opens the route upward?
   - What constitutes a completed Stratum and ordinary Cycle?
   - What happens at The Breach?
   - What makes an outcome a Coronation Failure?
   - What conditions make final atonement possible?
   - When do credits roll?
   - How can play continue without undoing the final release?

   Distinguish Attempt, Route, Rite of Passage, Stratum, Ascent, Coronation,
   Coronation Failure, Cycle, Final Atonement, and Echo. Give each a start,
   completion condition, failure condition, terminal transition, and
   persistence owner.

   In-world accounts may contradict why these things matter. They must not
   contradict the developer truth that the player is the former king, ordinary
   Cycles repeat his refusal, and final victory is accountability and release.
   The runtime and HUD must never contradict what the player needs to do next.


112. Give the current Ash-Walk build a real, reachable completion

   Turn the existing authored sequence into the first complete game-shaped
   loop:

   - Enter Ash-Walk.
   - Break the Last Chain by defeating the Ash-Warden.
   - Recover `Debt of the Last Keeper` from its stable authored source.
   - Commune with the First Anchor and deliberately bind, successfully mend, or
     turn away.
   - Interact with the awakened return gate.
   - Enter a `First Pilgrimage Remembered` completion state.

   The return gate should be dormant before those conditions are met. It should
   require deliberate interaction, state the next unmet pilgrimage objective,
   and never transition merely because the player walked near it.

   The completion state should:

   - Stop combat and prevent accidental transitions.
   - Name the completed pilgrimage and its authored reward.
   - Summarize route, time, deaths, Ash carried/bound, relics, and the chosen
     Anchor rite.
   - Record first completion and best clean completion without overwriting the
     current ascent journal prematurely.
   - Offer `Begin Another Echo` and `Return to Foundation Lab` for the prototype.
   - Reserve `Continue the Ascent` for the first real following Stratum.

   This is a vertical-slice completion, not a Coronation or false final credits.
   It gives every playtest a purpose and creates completion infrastructure the
   full game will later reuse.


113. Build a layered goal hierarchy

   Track goals at four readable scales:

   - Campaign: learn enough across failed Cycles to face judgment, pay the
     king's debt, and release the Cenotaph.
   - Ascent/Stratum: complete the current Rite of Passage.
   - Route: survive or solve the chosen passage condition.
   - Immediate: the next nearby action the player can intentionally take.

   Show only the current primary objective during play. Put the larger ascent
   chain, optional discoveries, completed rites, and known route alternatives in
   the pause journal. Markers should support orientation, then fade when the
   world itself communicates the route.

   Objective state must survive death, transition, continue, and Cycle change
   according to explicit ownership rules.


114. Make progression through the mountain explicit

   Add a campaign topology graph above the level-route graph:

     Ash-Walk -> Ward of Irons -> Hanging Slums -> Sanctuary
     -> Gallery of Wind -> Mirror-Crust -> The Breach

   This canonical narrative progression is:

     Physical ruin -> purposeless authority -> abandoned subjects
     -> safety without belonging -> overwhelming scale -> recognition
     -> judgment

   Local returns and shortcuts may change, but the graph must define:

   - Required and optional Strata.
   - Entry and completion conditions.
   - Rites that unlock each upward passage.
   - Branches, returns, shortcuts, and no-return boundaries.
   - Which progress survives death, a failed Ascent, and a completed Cycle.
   - Which level transition is traversal and which is victory progression.

   A transition gate must never silently stand in for campaign logic. Validate
   that every required Stratum, Coronation outcome, and Final Release path is
   reachable from a clean profile.


115. Add reusable Rites of Passage

   Every Stratum needs a major objective that proves the player has engaged with
   its defining pressure. It should not always be `kill the boss`.

   Typed rite conditions can include:

   - Defeat or spare a named threat.
   - Survive an authored hazard procession.
   - Carry, surrender, or transform a cursed relic.
   - Bind, refuse, or alter an Anchor under a stated condition.
   - Complete a route-specific encounter or memory chain.
   - Reach a place while a Cycle mutation remains active.

   Rites should compose existing events, objectives, encounters, loot, dialogue,
   and mountain reactions. Each rite needs a clear unmet state, completion
   consequence, upward passage, save record, and alternate-Cycle hook.


116. Make The Breach a true final ascent

   The Breach should combine the player's accumulated build, route history,
   Anchor choices, and mountain memory into a final test. It needs:

   - A clearly signaled commitment or no-return threshold.
   - A final traversal problem, encounter sequence, and named threat or ritual
     opposition that use established mechanics.
   - Conditions influenced by earlier decisions without making one build or
     route secretly invalid.
   - A failure policy that distinguishes death during the final trial from a
     completed Coronation Failure that intentionally begins another Cycle.
   - A terminal transition into the reckoning outcome, not an unexplained reset.

   The final challenge should test what the player learned and built, not merely
   inflate health and damage.


117. Separate Coronation Failures from Final Atonement

   Reaching The Breach completes an ordinary Ascent, but it does not by itself
   win the campaign. The reckoning should evaluate how the Pilgrim behaved and
   what he understood.

   Ordinary Coronation Failures can reflect the explicit narrative spine:

   - Fleeing when remaining would require responsibility.
   - Choosing power over obligation.
   - Refusing the recovered identity.
   - Reclaiming authority without accepting guilt.
   - Destroying or abandoning a necessary witness.
   - Arriving without sufficient understanding.

   Each failure needs an authored cause, readable outcome, world scar, retained
   memory, new evidence or obligation, next-Cycle consequence, and atomic save.
   It should recognize the player's completed Ascent while making the unresolved
   failure emotionally and mechanically real.

   Final Atonement becomes possible only when the Pilgrim has recovered enough
   truth, witnessed the consequences, completed abandoned obligations, accepted
   his identity without reclaiming royal authority, relinquished something of
   real value, and remained when escape was easier. Do not reduce this to an
   ending-selection menu. The exact sin and payment wait for author approval.


118. Make atonement progression systemic without exposing a checklist

   Add a persistent, developer-inspectable atonement model for:

   - Truth recovered about the kingdom and missing king.
   - Consequences personally witnessed rather than merely collected.
   - Abandoned obligations completed across Strata and Cycles.
   - Choices that accept or refuse the Pilgrim's identity.
   - Choices that reject restored authority and self-serving power.
   - Meaningful relinquishments whose value depends on the current build or
     history.
   - Moments when the player remained, returned, or protected a witness instead
     of taking the easier escape.

   Do not display an `atonement percentage` or require a fixed pile of lore
   objects. Surface progress through changed encounters, relic memories, NPC
   recognition, architecture, Sanctuary contradictions, and Breach outcomes.
   Debug tools must still explain exactly why an outcome is or is not available.

   Ordinary Cycle failure is canon, but it must never feel like erased play.
   Every failure should preserve understanding, alter future possibility, and
   move the player closer to behaving unlike the king who fled.


119. Present each kind of completion honestly and economically

   Build one reusable outcome presentation with three authored scales:

   - `First Pilgrimage Remembered`: the Ash-Walk test slice is complete.
   - `Coronation Failure`: an ordinary Ascent reached judgment but repeated the
     king's failure, and a changed Cycle begins.
   - `Final Release`: the Pilgrim accepts judgment, pays for the sin, ends his
     recurrence, and lets the Cenotaph die.

   Compose these outcomes from systems a solo developer can sustain:

   - Authored mountain reaction and environment-state change.
   - Short dialogue, witness, or inscription sequence.
   - Deliberate silence or future authored recording, never a synthetic fanfare.
   - Outcome title and readable consequence text.
   - Ascent remembrance showing routes, rites, Anchors, named threats, relics,
     deaths, choices, obligations, and world changes.
   - Credits and a final ending memory for `Final Release`.

   After final credits, offer `Remember an Earlier Echo`, view credits/archive,
   or begin a new profile. Do not place the released Pilgrim back in Sanctuary
   or canonically start another Cycle after the mountain has died.


120. Validate, author, save, and test every path to victory

   Add versioned definitions for campaign goals, Rites of Passage, completion
   conditions, Coronation Failures, atonement state, and Final Release. Extend
   the editor with a progression graph, condition inspector, outcome preview,
   and play-from-milestone command.

   Validation should reject:

   - Missing, circular, contradictory, or unreachable required goals.
   - A gate whose completion conditions can never become true.
   - A required Coronation outcome with no valid ascent path.
   - Final Release becoming available without all authored atonement rules.
   - A required objective whose source can be permanently lost.
   - Victory actions without atomic save consequences.
   - Echo/archive states that cannot be loaded or migrated.

   Headless scenarios should complete and fail every Rite, finish Ash-Walk from
   a clean profile, reach each authored Coronation Failure, earn Final Release,
   reload before and after each outcome, and prove that final credits and
   post-release Echo access begin exactly once when intended.

   A milestone build is not game-shaped until a clean profile can understand
   its declared goal and reach its declared completion state.


121. Synchronize the live documentation around the narrative spine

   The current `README.md` adds authoritative developer-facing truth that the
   older live documents do not yet fully carry. Make one deliberate canon pass:

   - Update `LORE.md` to distinguish hidden author truth from contradictory
     in-world interpretations without revealing the truth too early in play.
   - Extend `SYSTEMS.md` with atonement, witness, obligation, Coronation Failure,
     Final Release, and post-release Echo ownership.
   - Extend `ROADMAP.md` beyond forever expansion with staged narrative proof:
     consequence, recognition, judgment, and final release.
   - Make each Stratum's content contract contribute a distinct step in the
     progression from physical ruin to judgment.
   - Create the referenced `docs/design/CENOTAPH_STRATA.md` or remove the stale
     reference until that document exists.
   - Update `docs/README.md` and add a Markdown link/reference check so the
     documentation map cannot silently point at missing canon.
   - Keep `docs/archive/` unchanged and clearly non-authoritative.

   Do not define the king's exact sin or final payment during this cleanup. The
   documentation should reserve those decisions explicitly for the author.


Thematic Cohesion Additions
---------------------------

These are implementation requirements for the thematic contract near the top
of this file. They apply across gameplay, content, presentation, and tools.

122. Add shared feature-theme profiles

   Add a small versioned `ThemeProfile` contract for meaningful systems, content
   definitions, and content packs. Do not attach one to every minor prop.

   Useful fields:

   - Stable theme profile ID.
   - Home Stratum and allowed Cycle mutations.
   - Narrative role: consequence, witness, temptation, obligation, recognition,
     judgment, release, or supporting atmosphere.
   - Player question or pressure the feature creates.
   - Primary symbol and optional supporting symbol.
   - Emotional contract emphasis: alone, small, forgotten, or a deliberate
     combination.
   - Spoiler/revelation tier.
   - Semantic HUD, ambience, VFX, dialogue, and mountain-answer hooks.
   - Remembrance category and persistence layer.
   - Developer-authored reference/authority note.

   Runtime systems consume semantic hooks; they do not infer art, prose, audio,
   geometry, or lore from the profile name.


123. Enforce the thematic integration chain

   Generate an inspectable chain for each meaningful feature:

     Mechanical Rule -> World Source -> Player Decision
     -> Mountain Answer -> Remembrance

   Examples of valid ownership:

   - An Anchor rite changes resources and respawn, exists at an authored Anchor,
     asks for a deliberate choice, makes the mountain answer, and records the
     chosen relation to obligation.
   - A named relic changes combat, comes from a stable keeper or place, tempts a
     build decision, alters presentation when active, and carries provenance
     into later Cycles.

   A feature report should identify missing links without forcing theme onto
   mouse sensitivity, file recovery, graphics settings, or other purely
   technical functions.


124. Add symbol ownership and anti-dilution rules

   Create validated semantic symbol IDs for Ash, Chain, Bell, Mirror, Wind,
   Silence, Gold, Anchor Light, Blood/Ember, Root/Machinery, and Waystone.

   Each symbol definition can specify:

   - Mechanical and narrative meanings.
   - Preferred and reserved Strata.
   - Compatible status, route, encounter, relic, and rite roles.
   - Semantic color, ambience, VFX, and text hooks.
   - Incompatible combinations and overuse warnings.

   Author overrides remain possible with an explicit reason. Validation should
   catch accidental motif soup, not prevent a deliberate contradiction. Symbol
   metadata never authorizes generated visual forms or synthetic sounds.


125. Add spoiler and revelation tiers

   Give objectives, dialogue, relic memories, encounters, discoveries, world
   events, and outcome presentations an authored maximum revelation tier:

   - Surface: physical survival and immediately observable rules.
   - Consequence: abandoned lives and systems left by an absent authority.
   - Contradiction: incompatible testimony and repeating history.
   - Recognition: evidence that turns toward the Pilgrim's identity.
   - Judgment: the king's guilt, obligations, and possible atonement.
   - Author Truth: developer-only facts not yet permitted in player content.

   Strata and Cycle milestones define which tiers may appear. Validators should
   reject an early random drop, mutator, editor default, or generic dialogue
   pool that reveals a later truth. Debug tools may override the gate explicitly.


126. Separate action text, mechanical text, and memory text

   Player-facing definitions should provide distinct authored fields:

   - `display_name`: the stable authored identity.
   - `action_text`: direct instruction such as `BREAK THE LAST CHAIN`.
   - `mechanical_text`: exact cost, effect, duration, requirement, or failure.
   - `memory_text`: optional implication, testimony, or remembered consequence.

   HUD surfaces choose the smallest set needed for the current decision. The
   Reliquary and journal may show all layers. Localization, text fitting, and
   accessibility alternatives apply independently to each field.

   Never generate memory text from an ID, and never use memory text as the only
   explanation of a mechanic.


127. Build thematic provenance through rewards and progression

   For every named relic, major perk rite, shortcut payment, and significant Ash
   reward, record:

   - Who, what, or where it came from.
   - Why that source could possess or manifest it.
   - What behavior, temptation, or debt it carries.
   - Which choice granted, refused, transformed, or lost it.
   - How later Cycles may contradict or reinterpret it.

   Use this graph in the Reliquary, Remembrance, loot-source reports, duplicate
   outcomes, NPC recognition, and atonement logic. Common Ash can stay concise;
   named rewards must never feel detached from the encounter or world state that
   produced them.


128. Theme Cycle mutations as remembered causes

   Every Cycle mutation should define:

   - Cause: what prior failure, choice, scar, or unresolved obligation it echoes.
   - Distortion: what the mountain remembers incorrectly.
   - Rule: the exact gameplay change.
   - Counterplay: how the player can understand and adapt.
   - Evidence: how the change appears in world state, routes, enemies, relics,
     dialogue, or ambience.
   - Remembrance: what this Cycle records after the player responds.

   The Cycle Director may recombine authored mutations, but its player-facing
   output should read like recurrence with a cause, not a weekly modifier card.
   Difficulty scaling alone is never a complete mutation.


129. Choreograph mountain answers instead of reward effects

   Give major decisions a response envelope:

     Anticipation -> Decision -> Immediate Answer -> Decay
     -> Persistent Consequence or Delayed Echo

   Choose only the channels the moment needs:

   - World transform or route change.
   - Light, fog, wind, or continuous ambience shift.
   - Bounded semantic particles.
   - Enemy/encounter response.
   - Short dialogue, inscription, or silence.
   - HUD confirmation and later Remembrance.

   Not every answer should be loud or immediate. Silence, no visible forgiveness,
   or a consequence in a later Stratum may be the authored response. Answers
   must preserve combat readability, respect presentation budgets, and remain
   silent when approved recordings do not exist.


130. Add thematic linting and playtest gates

   Extend validation/editor reports with warnings for:

   - Missing world source, player decision, answer, or Remembrance.
   - Generic player-facing names where an authored identity is required.
   - Flavor text used instead of mechanical explanation.
   - Primary symbols used outside their semantic role or too frequently.
   - Late-Stratum motifs or spoilers appearing too early.
   - Named rewards with no provenance relationship to their source.
   - Cycle mutations with no remembered cause or counterplay.
   - Mountain answers that use every presentation channel at once.
   - Themed accessibility, error, or save language that reduces clarity.

   Add five questions to milestone playtests:

   - What did this feature ask you to do?
   - What choice did you make and why?
   - What in the world seemed to own or cause it?
   - How did the mountain answer, and what changed afterward?
   - Were the rule and cost clear before you committed?

   Reports reveal gaps; they do not generate lore or declare artistic approval.

What To Avoid For Now
---------------------

Avoid these until Ash-Walk is replayable and the runtime state is cleaner:

- Seven complete Strata.
- Hundreds of relics.
- Full crafting.
- Multiplayer.
- Market, trader, stash, insurance, or extraction-style systems.
- Huge open maps.
- Production enemy model volume before enemy roles are fun.
- Large cinematic story delivery.
- Complex NPC schedules.
- Generic survival meters such as hunger and thirst.
- A wholesale ECS rewrite before runtime ownership requirements are proven.
- A full procedural-terrain generator instead of modular authored routes.
- An unrestricted scripting language before typed event actions are exhausted.
- A giant boss framework before the Ash-Warden proves encounter lifecycle.
- A full rendering rewrite before captures and GPU timings identify the need.
- Production-looking generated models, silhouettes, ornament, or anatomy without
  developer-supplied visual direction.
- Synthesized one-shot sounds standing in for authored recordings.
- Editor polish that delays useful validation, placement, and playtest workflow.
- Treating endless Cycles as an excuse to deny the player a real victory.
- Treating a completed ordinary Ascent as erased or meaningless because its
  Coronation failed.
- Calling a Coronation Failure the final victory or rolling final credits before
  the Pilgrim has earned atonement.
- Canonically starting another Cycle after Final Release has ended recurrence
  and allowed the Cenotaph to die.
- Requiring all seven production Strata before the prototype gets a completion
  state.
- Letting proximity-only level gates bypass objectives, rites, or deliberate
  interaction.
- Replacing every familiar action with opaque ritual language.
- Calling a feature themed because it has ash particles, gold trim, and a lore
  paragraph while its mechanic leaves no world consequence.
- Using Ash, chains, bells, mirrors, wind, silence, and royal imagery in every
  Stratum until none of them communicates anything.
- Spending Mirror-Crust recognition or Breach judgment imagery in early
  Ash-Walk content for spectacle.
- Making accessibility, settings, save recovery, errors, or developer tools
  cryptic in the name of atmosphere.
- Generating flavor prose, production art, voices, music, or the king's exact
  sin from semantic theme tags.


The Dream Version
-----------------

The dream version of Cenotaph is not just "a shooter with loot."

It is a game where:

- The player survives by adapting, not by optimizing a spreadsheet.
- The player unknowingly climbs through the consequences of his own abandoned
  kingdom.
- Relics are useful memories with teeth.
- Anchors feel like ritual claims against a hostile world.
- Routes are dangerous decisions.
- Enemies attack the player's habits and options.
- The mountain changes because of what happened.
- Mechanics, world source, player choice, mountain answer, and Remembrance tell
  the same thematic story.
- Replays feel different through relics, threats, routes, world state, and
  memory.
- Ordinary Cycles fail in ways that reveal what the Pilgrim still refuses to
  understand, accept, complete, or surrender.
- The final victory comes from becoming answerable to the throne without
  reclaiming it, remaining for judgment, and releasing the kingdom from its
  unfinished death.
- The core remains small enough that one developer can keep adding strange,
  beautiful, dangerous things for years.

Build the mountain so it can remember.
Then make sure it remembers incorrectly.
Finally, let it die when the Pilgrim remembers enough to remain.
