CENOTAPH ENEMY MODEL GENERATOR BRIEF

Purpose

This file describes enemy 3D model directions for Cenotaph: The Great Omission.
Use it as a generator brief for game-ready enemy meshes, material prompts, and
silhouette references.

Gameplay-first enemy roles, counterplay, and encounter usage live in
ENEMY_GAMEPLAY_ROSTER.txt. This file should follow that roster.

Important context:
- Starter enemy definitions exist for Ashbound, Burdened, Censer, Chainrunner,
  and Harpy.
- Starter enemy definitions currently use low-poly authored silhouette meshes
  under `assets/enemies/`.
- The baseline runtime behavior is activation-gated chase and wind-up melee
  attack; production visual identity and animation readability still need real
  authored models.
- All enemies must fit the same visual language: mountain ruin, ritual decay,
  heavy pressure, readable silhouettes, and weathered material surfaces.

Global Model Rules

- Keep models game-ready, not cinematic.
- Prioritize silhouette clarity from a first-person view.
- Use asymmetry, worn edges, dents, tears, and patched materials.
- Favor stone, rusted iron, old cloth, leather, bone, soot, and tarnished brass.
- Avoid bright fantasy colors, clean surfaces, or heroic proportions.
- Make the enemy body easy to read at distance and while moving.
- Leave room for animation and gameplay readability.
- If the model has attachments, make them feel fused, carried, or badly repaired.

1. Ashbound

Gameplay role:
Grunt and baseline melee pressure.

Model direction:
- Thin, ash-covered pilgrim or half-collapsed humanoid.
- Simple readable body shape: head, torso, arms, legs.
- Hunched posture, forward lean, and low-status silhouette.
- Should look dangerous in groups but not elite alone.

Materials and details:
- Ash-stained cloth, cracked skin or stone, simple straps, broken pilgrim gear.
- Optional small blade, clawed hands, or splintered tool.

Animation feeling:
- Direct jog, stumble, swipe, recover.

2. Burdened

Gameplay role:
Tank and route blocker.

Model direction:
- Hunched humanoid or near-humanoid body.
- Broad shoulders, heavy back, thick legs, and a weighted center of gravity.
- The silhouette should look overloaded, as if carrying stone, debris, or a
  fused burden that has become part of its frame.
- Head should be partially hidden, masked, or compressed into the posture.
- Hands should be large and worn, with a gripping or dragging stance.

Materials and details:
- Stone plates, rusted braces, rope lashings, torn pilgrim cloth, soot.
- Cracks, scars, and patched seams.
- Optional cargo or burden growth fused to the shoulders or spine.

Animation feeling:
- Slow steps, heavy turns, shoulder sway, grinding weight.

Generator note:
- This is the current canonical foundation enemy. The first production model
  should preserve the readable wide, overloaded silhouette from the low-poly
  gameplay placeholder.

3. Censer

Gameplay role:
Glass cannon and fragile high-damage attacker.

Model direction:
- Thin ritual attacker with a dangerous object carried forward.
- Body should look frail, but the weapon/focus should look volatile.
- Use a smoking censer, cracked lamp, ritual firearm, ash vessel, or heat core.
- Silhouette should make the wind-up threat obvious.

Materials and details:
- Burned cloth, brass, ceramic, ash residue, ember cracks, blackened hands.
- Add vents, smoke trails, glowing cracks, or suspended ash fragments sparingly.

Animation feeling:
- Slow aim, visible charge-up, sharp release, vulnerable recovery.

4. Chainrunner

Gameplay role:
High-speed flanker.

Model direction:
- Lean, long-legged, and tense.
- Wrapped limbs, exposed joints, and loose chain trails.
- Body should imply sudden lateral motion rather than brute strength.
- Keep profile narrow so it reads as fast, not bulky.

Materials and details:
- Chain links, torn cloth wraps, leather binding, rust, pale ash markings.
- Avoid heavy armor.

Animation feeling:
- Sprint, skid, lunge, recoil, circle.

5. Harpy

Gameplay role:
Aerial and vertical threat.

Model direction:
- Lean, avian, and predatory.
- Long limbs that can read as wings, climbing arms, or both.
- Hooked feet, narrow ribcage, and a stretched neck or masked head.
- The silhouette should read clearly from below, above, and at a distance.

Materials and details:
- Weathered feathers, torn membranes, bone, leather straps, ash-gray tissue.
- Optional wing claws, perch hooks, or talon-like hands.
- Keep the form agile rather than bulky.

Animation feeling:
- Perch, launch, glide, circle, strike, and retreat.

6. Bellworn

Gameplay role:
Ranged harasser.

Model direction:
- Medium-sized ranged pilgrim with a clear firing posture.
- Should read as less explosive than the Censer but more persistent.
- Bell-mask, shoulder chime, old firearm, or compact relic launcher can carry
  the ranged identity.

Materials and details:
- Tarnished brass, worn cloth, small bells, iron sights, cracked wood.
- Keep the silhouette upright enough to distinguish from melee grunts.

Animation feeling:
- Aim, fire, step sideways, re-aim, keep pressure.

7. Silencer

Gameplay role:
Suppressor.

Model direction:
- Tall, narrow, and quiet-looking.
- The silhouette should feel compressed and controlled, with a muted posture.
- Head design should suggest sound reduction: sealed mouth, mask, hood, veil,
  or a hollow face cavity.
- Use long forearms, tucked elbows, and a restrained stance.

Materials and details:
- Matte cloth, dark felt, bone, tarnished metal, wrapped cords.
- Small resonant elements should be minimal or absent.
- Optional throat plates, bell fragments, or sealed vents that imply it steals
  or blocks noise.

Animation feeling:
- Stillness, short gliding motions, deliberate gestures.

8. Paranoiac

Gameplay role:
Escalator and alarm enemy.

Model direction:
- Angular and tense, as if always about to alert something else.
- Sharp shoulders, uneven limbs, and a posture that leans forward.
- Add repeated or doubled features to suggest nervous repetition:
  clustered eyes, split masks, antenna-like protrusions, or alarm fins.
- The body should look restless even when idle.

Materials and details:
- Cracked plaster, iron bands, chain fragments, warning marks, dull glass.
- Small bell shapes, spikes, or signal pieces are acceptable if they help the
  "this will call trouble" reading.

Animation feeling:
- Jittery scanning, sudden turns, alarm wind-up.

9. Anchor Parasite

Gameplay role:
Swarmer and small pressure enemy.

Model direction:
- Small to medium parasitic mass that clings to anchors, walls, or machinery.
- Should feel like something that feeds on stability.
- Include hooks, tendrils, or growths that attach to surfaces.

Materials and details:
- Wet stone, root-like tissue, rust stains, tar, grime.
- The body should look invasive and anchored, not free-roaming.

Animation feeling:
- Scuttle, cling, leap, swarm, detach.

10. Chain Pilgrim

Gameplay role:
Planned humanoid pressure enemy or Chainrunner variant source.

Model direction:
- A pilgrim silhouette wrapped in chains, bindings, and hanging metal lengths.
- The body should feel ritualistic and exhausted, like a broken traveler that
  became part of the mountain's mechanism.
- Good model cues are bowed shoulders, a wrapped torso, and dragging chain mass.

Materials and details:
- Iron chain, frayed cloth, old travel gear, prayer tags, corrosion.

Animation feeling:
- Drag, stagger, whip-chain, lunge.

11. Root-Machine Hybrid

Gameplay role:
Controller and arena-denial enemy.

Model direction:
- Organic root growth fused into industrial machinery.
- The model should feel half living, half mechanical, with visible tension
  between those two systems.
- Use branching roots, pipes, joints, pistons, and asymmetrical block forms.
- Add a readable core that can become a weak point later.

Materials and details:
- Bark, root fibers, rivets, gears, rust, oil stains, damp earth.

Animation feeling:
- Plant, unfold, pulse, lock down, release hazards.

12. Mirror of the Player

Gameplay role:
Late-game duelist and mimic.

Model direction:
- Human-shaped mirror threat that copies the player's broad silhouette without
  becoming a literal duplicate.
- Use reflective or glass-like elements in a damaged, irregular way.
- Keep the body readable as an imitation, but uncanny and slightly wrong.

Materials and details:
- Cracked glass, polished metal, pale bone, reflected fragments, distorted seams.

Animation feeling:
- Dash, pause, copy rhythm, punish, retreat.

13. Bell-Headed

Gameplay role:
Elite modifier frame.

Model direction:
- Stronger, more ceremonial silhouette than a normal enemy.
- Head should clearly read as bell-like, mask-like, or sacred metal housing.
- Bulkier armor, stronger posture, and more authoritative geometry than the
  normal version of the base enemy.

Materials and details:
- Brass, iron, dark lacquer, ceremonial cloth, dents, etched symbols.

Animation feeling:
- Slower confidence, stronger tells, ritual charge-up.

Variant Overlays

Use these as modular variant directions instead of separate base models when
possible:

- Burning Burdened: charred cloth, ember cracks, smoke-darkened stone.
- Armored Burdened: heavier plates, brackets, bolts, reinforced shoulders.
- Silent Paranoiac: more muted surfaces, wrapped mouth, fewer alarm details.
- Mirror Harpy: reflective feather surfaces, broken glass accents, pale sheen.
- Void-Touched Silencer: black core surfaces, dim highlights, soundless shape.
- Frenzied Harpy: sharper claws, more aggressive posture, torn membranes.

Final Generator Notes

- Every enemy should have a unique silhouette first, then surface detail.
- Match the gameplay role before adding ornament.
- Keep the enemy language consistent with Cenotaph's mountain, ruin, and ritual
  themes.
- When in doubt, make the model heavier, older, stranger, and more worn.
- Do not make enemies heroic, cute, or clean.
