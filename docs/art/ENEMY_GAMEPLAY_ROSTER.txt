CENOTAPH ENEMY GAMEPLAY ROSTER

Purpose

This file rethinks Cenotaph enemies by gameplay role first.
The goal is not "many monsters." The goal is a readable enemy ecosystem where
each enemy asks the player a different survival question during the climb.

Core Rule

Every enemy must have:
- A clear combat job.
- A clear silhouette.
- A clear warning tell.
- A clear counterplay option.
- A reason to exist in an encounter group.

If two enemies create the same player response, merge them or turn one into a
modifier instead of a base enemy.

Recommended Core Roster

1. Grunt: Ashbound

Gameplay role:
Basic melee enemy and baseline combat test.

Player question:
Can you move, aim, manage distance, and finish a simple threat cleanly?

Behavior:
- Walks or jogs toward the player.
- Uses a short melee swipe.
- Low health, low damage, low complexity.
- Dangerous in groups, weak alone.

Counterplay:
- Backpedal, sidestep, shoot, dash away, use terrain.

Encounter use:
- Spawn in groups of 2 to 5.
- Use as filler pressure around stronger enemies.
- Best for early Ash-Walk and tutorial combat.

Model read:
Thin pilgrim shape, ash-covered, hunched, simple weapon or clawed hands.

2. Tank: Burdened

Gameplay role:
Slow durable route blocker.

Player question:
Can you reposition before the heavy enemy closes the space?

Behavior:
- Moves slowly.
- Has high health.
- Uses heavy melee swings or body pressure.
- Occupies routes, chokepoints, bridges, and doorways.
- Should feel impossible to ignore but easy to outmaneuver in open space.

Counterplay:
- Kite it, dash around it, use elevation, kill smaller threats first.

Encounter use:
- One Burdened plus several grunts is a clean early encounter.
- Two Burdened should be rare and deliberate.
- Armored Burdened can be an elite modifier later.

Model read:
Large hunched body, heavy back burden, stone/iron weight, slow stance.

3. Glass Cannon: Censer

Gameplay role:
Fragile high-damage attacker.

Player question:
Can you identify and kill the dangerous fragile target before it fires?

Behavior:
- Low health.
- Slow or medium movement.
- Long wind-up before a high-damage attack.
- Can use projectile, beam, explosive ash burst, or line attack.
- Very dangerous if ignored.

Counterplay:
- Break line of sight, focus fire, interrupt during wind-up, dash sideways.

Encounter use:
- Place behind grunts or a Burdened.
- Do not spam them early.
- Works well in overlooks, ledges, ruins, and long corridors.

Model read:
Thin body carrying a smoking censer, cracked lamp, ritual firearm, or ash focus.

4. High-Speed: Chainrunner

Gameplay role:
Fast flanker and panic enemy.

Player question:
Can you track a fast threat without losing awareness of the arena?

Behavior:
- Fast movement.
- Low to medium health.
- Dashes or lunges in short bursts.
- Circles the player instead of walking straight in.
- Attacks, retreats, then attacks again.

Counterplay:
- Listen for audio tell, dodge the lunge, punish recovery, use close-range burst.

Encounter use:
- Add one Chainrunner to make a simple fight nervous.
- Pairs well with Burdened because the tank holds attention.
- Avoid large packs until movement and camera feel very stable.

Model read:
Lean chained pilgrim, long legs, wrapped limbs, loose chain trails.

5. Aerial: Harpy

Gameplay role:
Vertical threat and anti-climb pressure.

Player question:
Can you look up, use cover, and manage vertical danger while climbing?

Behavior:
- Flies, perches, or leaps between high points.
- Uses dive attacks, ranged screeches, or claw passes.
- Punishes exposed bridges, ledges, and open climbs.
- Should retreat upward after attacking.

Counterplay:
- Use cover, wait for dive, shoot during approach or recovery.

Encounter use:
- First appears alone in Ash-Walk.
- Later appears in pairs or with ground pressure.
- Essential for Hanging Slums and exposed high routes.

Model read:
Lean winged or climbing body, hooked feet, clear airborne silhouette.

6. Ranged Harasser: Bellworn

Gameplay role:
Persistent ranged chip damage.

Player question:
Can you close distance or use cover without becoming reckless?

Behavior:
- Medium health.
- Medium range.
- Fires repeated low-to-medium damage shots.
- Repositions slowly between attacks.
- Less bursty than the Censer, but more consistent.

Counterplay:
- Break line of sight, close distance, stagger it, use terrain.

Encounter use:
- Good for teaching cover and route choice.
- Pairs well with grunts.
- Should not be used too much in cramped rooms.

Model read:
Bell-mask or old pilgrim shooter, shoulder-mounted chime weapon, clear ranged pose.

7. Suppressor: Silencer

Gameplay role:
Disables or weakens player tools.

Player question:
Can you adapt when your preferred tool is unreliable?

Behavior:
- Creates a suppression field or targeted silence effect.
- May weaken dash, sprint, relic effects, healing, or UI certainty.
- Low to medium health.
- Tries to stay behind other enemies.

Counterplay:
- Leave the field, focus the Silencer, use basic movement and weapon skill.

Encounter use:
- Introduce only after the player understands the suppressed tool.
- Best as a rare priority target, not common filler.
- Strong in Ward of Irons and Mirror-Crust.

Model read:
Tall narrow body, sealed mouth or mask, muted cloth, quiet posture.

8. Escalator: Paranoiac

Gameplay role:
Alarm enemy that makes fights worse over time.

Player question:
Can you prioritize a non-lethal but strategically dangerous target?

Behavior:
- Scans, shrieks, rings, signals, or reveals the player.
- Calls reinforcements or buffs nearby enemies if not stopped.
- Low to medium health.
- Avoids direct combat when possible.

Counterplay:
- Kill quickly, interrupt alarm, break line of sight before it signals.

Encounter use:
- Great for route-risk rooms and hidden dwellings.
- One Paranoiac changes a fight; multiple should be rare.
- Strong fit for Hanging Slums and alarm-chain events.

Model read:
Angular restless body, repeated eyes or split mask, alarm shapes.

9. Swarmer: Anchor Parasite

Gameplay role:
Small pressure enemy that punishes tunnel vision.

Player question:
Can you clear small threats before they drain health or block movement?

Behavior:
- Small, quick, low health.
- Attacks in packs.
- May cling, slow, bite, or explode after a delay.
- Not individually scary, but dangerous while fighting bigger enemies.

Counterplay:
- Area attacks, quick melee, movement, target switching.

Encounter use:
- Use around Anchors, roots, dead relics, and corrupted machinery.
- Good as a supporting enemy, not a main duel.

Model read:
Small hooked parasite, tendrils, root-stone body, clinging posture.

10. Controller: Root-Machine Hybrid

Gameplay role:
Zone denial and arena control.

Player question:
Can you move through a changing space without getting trapped?

Behavior:
- Creates roots, slows, barricades, shock lines, steam vents, or hazard patches.
- Medium to high health.
- Low movement.
- Dangerous because it changes the terrain problem.

Counterplay:
- Watch ground tells, relocate, destroy the core, use vertical routes.

Encounter use:
- Use in machinery rooms, root halls, and Breach spaces.
- Pairs well with ranged enemies because it limits movement.
- Should be uncommon because it changes encounter complexity.

Model read:
Organic root mass fused into machinery, visible core, pipes and branches.

11. Duelist: Mirror of the Player

Gameplay role:
Late-game mimic and skill check.

Player question:
Can you fight a corrupted version of your own movement and attack rhythm?

Behavior:
- Copies broad player behavior without matching every build detail.
- Uses dash, ranged attack, and retreat patterns.
- Medium health, high threat.
- Should feel uncanny, not unfair.

Counterplay:
- Bait dash, punish recovery, break rhythm, use terrain.

Encounter use:
- Save for Mirror-Crust or special events.
- Works best as a solo or elite encounter.
- Do not use as common filler.

Model read:
Humanoid reflection, cracked glass, almost-player silhouette, wrong proportions.

12. Elite Frame: Bell-Headed

Gameplay role:
Elite shell applied to another role.

Player question:
Can you handle a familiar enemy after one rule changes?

Behavior:
- Not a basic enemy role by itself.
- Use as an elite modifier carrier.
- Can add armor, aura, command, stagger resistance, or a special attack.

Counterplay:
- Depends on the base enemy.
- Must have a stronger tell than the normal version.

Encounter use:
- Bell-Headed Burdened: elite tank.
- Bell-Headed Silencer: stronger suppression elite.
- Bell-Headed Paranoiac: dangerous alarm elite.

Model read:
Ceremonial bell head, stronger posture, more metal, clear elite silhouette.

Recommended Implementation Order

1. Ashbound
Basic grunt. This gives combat a default enemy that is easier to tune than the
Burdened.

2. Burdened
Tank and route blocker. Already named in current content and should become the
first strong identity enemy.

3. Censer
Glass cannon. Adds target priority and line-of-sight play.

4. Chainrunner
High-speed flanker. Tests movement, dash, audio tells, and camera feel.

5. Harpy
Aerial enemy. Turns the vertical climb into combat, not just scenery.

6. Paranoiac
Escalator. Makes encounter priority matter.

7. Silencer
Suppression. Add this after dash, stamina, relics, and healing are stable enough
that disabling or weakening them is meaningful.

First Ash-Walk Enemy Set

For the first complete playable Ash-Walk slice, use:
- Ashbound as the common grunt.
- Burdened as the tank.
- Censer as the fragile high-damage enemy.
- Harpy as the first vertical threat.
- One elite variant, preferably Armored Burdened or Burning Burdened.

Avoid adding Silencer, Mirror of the Player, and Root-Machine Hybrid too early.
They depend on systems that should be stable first.

Encounter Recipes

Simple pressure:
- 3 Ashbound.

Route blocker:
- 1 Burdened.
- 2 Ashbound.

Priority target:
- 1 Censer.
- 3 Ashbound.

Movement stress:
- 1 Chainrunner.
- 2 Ashbound.

Vertical stress:
- 1 Harpy.
- 1 Burdened.

Alarm room:
- 1 Paranoiac.
- 2 Ashbound.
- 1 Burdened if the room is large.

Suppression room:
- 1 Silencer.
- 2 Ashbound.
- 1 Censer or Bellworn.

Design Warnings

- Do not make every enemy fast. Speed loses meaning if everything rushes.
- Do not make every enemy durable. Health should communicate role.
- Do not add suppression before the player has tools worth suppressing.
- Do not add aerial packs until aiming and cover feel fair.
- Do not use glass cannons without readable wind-up tells.
- Do not make elites by only adding health. Change one behavior rule.

Naming Notes

These names are working names except for Burdened, Harpy, Silencer, Paranoiac,
and Mirror of the Player, which already exist in the project docs.
Rename freely if a better Cenotaph name appears, but keep the gameplay roles
stable so the combat ecosystem remains understandable.
