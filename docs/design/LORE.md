# Cenotaph Lore and Tone Guide

## Purpose

This document protects the dreamlike identity of Cenotaph.

It is allowed to be stranger, more poetic, and less mechanical than the other documents.

However, lore should still support the game.

The dream can be surreal.

The player objective must remain clear.

---

# Core Myth

The world is missing something.

No one remembers what was removed.

The mountain is the shape left behind by that absence.

This absence is called:

```text
The Great Omission
```

The player climbs through the consequence of something reality cannot remember.

---

# Tone Statement

Cenotaph should feel:

- Surreal
- Sacred
- Oppressive
- Beautiful
- Decayed
- Strange
- Lonely
- Violent when necessary
- Dreamlike but playable

Cenotaph should avoid feeling:

- Random for the sake of randomness
- Comedic like Borderlands
- Pure power fantasy
- Generic grimdark
- Generic movement shooter
- Generic fantasy dungeon crawler
- Over-explained

---

# Narrative Philosophy

The story is discovered, not explained.

The Great Omission should remain mysterious.

The player should understand enough to care, but never enough to fully solve the world.

A good Cenotaph mystery creates this feeling:

```text
I do not understand this place yet, but I believe it has rules.
```

A bad Cenotaph mystery creates this feeling:

```text
This is random nonsense.
```

---

# Writing Rules

- Avoid direct exposition.
- Preserve mystery.
- Let item descriptions carry strange implications.
- Let the world contradict itself carefully.
- Use symbolism, but keep gameplay readable.
- The dream can be strange, but the objective must be clear.
- The mountain should feel older than memory.
- The player should rarely receive complete answers.
- Relics should sound useful and cursed at the same time.
- NPCs may lie, forget, repeat, or speak from impossible positions.

---

# The Mountain

The mountain is the protagonist.

It is not alive in a simple biological sense.

It is not only a place.

It is pressure, memory, architecture, judgment, and wound.

The mountain:

- Forgets
- Rearranges
- Suppresses
- Reveals
- Punishes stillness
- Rewards adaptation
- Preserves fragments incorrectly

The mountain should never feel fully understood.

---

# The Climb

The player climbs because remaining below is impossible.

The climb is:

- Survival
- Pilgrimage
- Escape
- Search
- Punishment
- Inheritance
- Repetition

The player should not climb only for loot.

The player climbs to continue existing.

---

# Relics

Relics are memories made physical.

A relic may be:

- A weapon
- A tool
- A burden
- A witness
- A lie that became useful
- A fragment of a forgotten person
- A rule that survived after reality forgot its source

Relics should feel powerful, strange, and incomplete.

Example tone:

```text
It sings only where speech has failed.
```

```text
The barrel is warm with someone else's last sunrise.
```

```text
It remembers a battle that never happened, and still reloads for it.
```

---

# The Great Omission

The Great Omission should not be fully explained early.

Possible interpretations:

- A god was removed.
- A kingdom erased itself.
- The summit deleted the world below.
- Memory failed before matter did.
- The mountain is a scar from an impossible choice.
- The player is climbing through the absence of an ending.
- The world is repeating because something refused to be remembered.

None of these must be confirmed.

Contradictions are allowed if they feel intentional.

---

# King Crimson-Inspired Dream Logic

The inspiration is not direct copying.

The useful influence is the feeling of:

- Reality obeying symbolic rules.
- Cause and effect feeling slightly wrong.
- Strange images treated as normal.
- Mystery presented with confidence.
- Violence and beauty existing side by side.
- Time, memory, identity, and fate bending around obsession.

Good dream logic:

```text
A cathedral hangs upside down. Nobody comments on it.
```

```text
A dead pilgrim thanks the player for a rescue that has not happened yet.
```

```text
A mirror shows the room after the player leaves it.
```

```text
A boss remembers dying in a Cycle the player has not reached.
```

Bad dream logic:

```text
Random objects appear with no pattern, consequence, or emotional meaning.
```

---

# Narrative Delivery Methods

Use:

- Relic descriptions
- Environmental storytelling
- NPC fragments
- Hidden inscriptions
- Boss memories
- Cycle changes
- Dreamlike encounters
- Contradictory accounts
- Architecture that implies history
- Repeated symbols

Avoid:

- Long exposition dumps
- Explaining the entire setting
- Overly literal lore documents in-game
- NPCs who fully understand the world
- Quest text that breaks the dream tone

---

# Recurring Symbols

Use and expand this list over time.

## Ash

Memory reduced to weather.

## Chains

Stability, imprisonment, forced ascent.

## Bells

Warnings, rituals, dead cities calling.

## Mirrors

False memory, delayed truth, alternate paths.

## Roots

Old life fused with stone and machinery.

## Cathedrals

Sacred architecture after faith has failed.

## Machinery

Purpose continuing after meaning is gone.

## Wind

Exposure, height, unseen force.

## Silence

Suppression, loss of agency, prayer removed.

## Gold

Old authority, tarnished divinity, beautiful failure.

# NPC Tone

NPCs should feel like:

- Pilgrims
- Echoes
- Survivors
- Things pretending to be people
- People who have forgotten what they survived
- People who remember events incorrectly
- People who know one true thing and many false things

NPCs should rarely provide complete certainty.

---

# Environmental Storytelling Rules

A good environment should answer one question and ask two more.

Example:

The player finds a dining hall suspended vertically against a cliff.

It answers:

```text
People lived here.
```

It asks:

```text
Why did gravity change?
Why are the plates nailed to the table?
```
